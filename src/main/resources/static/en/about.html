Who was Ray Peat?

Raymond Peat (1936–2022) was an American biologist with a PhD from the University of Oregon (1972), specialising in physiology. He began studying progesterone and related hormones in 1968, and spent the rest of his life researching how hormones, food and the environment change metabolism.

He taught at several universities in the United States and Mexico, worked as a nutrition counsellor, and wrote five books and dozens of articles that he published for free on his website. For years he also answered listeners' questions on more than two hundred radio shows and podcasts. His work is still read and argued over in forums and on social media.

His ideas in a nutshell

For Peat, health depends above all on how much energy your cells produce. His best-known positions follow from that, and many go against today's medical consensus:

  • The thyroid sets the pace of metabolism; he judged it by temperature and pulse, and distrusted relying on TSH alone.
  • Sugar and fruit were not the enemy: he defended them over starch.
  • Polyunsaturated vegetable oils (PUFA) slow the thyroid and promote inflammation and ageing.
  • Stress and cortisol are what wears the body down, and glucose helps hold them back.
  • Estrogen acts as a stress hormone, and progesterone protects against it.
  • Serotonin is not “the happiness hormone” but a mediator of stress.
  • Carbon dioxide (CO2) is not just waste: it protects cells.
  • Cholesterol is a protective molecule, and it rises when the thyroid is slow.

What did he eat and recommend?

He left no fixed diet, but his preferences are clear: milk and cheese, ripe fruit and orange juice, gelatin and broths, eggs, shellfish and liver now and then, coffee with meals, salt to taste, and coconut oil or butter for cooking. He gave less room to starch and grains and to muscle meat without gelatin to balance it. The summary is in the Ray Peat diet, and there are 28 topics explained with their sources.

His books

He wrote five: Mind and Tissue, Progesterone in Orthomolecular Medicine, Nutrition for Women, Generative Energy and From PMS to Menopause. The chatbot uses them to answer and cites them by page, without showing their text.

Frequently asked questions

Was Ray Peat a medical doctor?

No. He held a PhD in biology, not a medical degree, and worked as a researcher, teacher and nutrition counsellor. That is why he is sometimes called “Dr. Ray Peat”.

What is the bioenergetic view?

It is the usual name for his way of understanding health: the body works well when its cells produce plenty of energy, and it gets sick or ages when that production falls. He cared about the thyroid, sugar, hormones and stress because of how they raise or lower that energy.

What does “peating” mean?

It is the word the online community uses for following Ray Peat's ideas in food and habits. He never sold a method or a fixed diet.
