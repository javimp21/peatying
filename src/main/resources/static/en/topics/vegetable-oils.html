Ray Peat on vegetable oils

For Ray Peat, vegetable oils rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) were toxic by their very chemical nature. He held that they slow down enzymes that digest proteins and that take part in making thyroid hormone, and that they promote inflammation, ageing and cancer 1. He also thought their popularity owed more to industry marketing than to solid evidence 12.

Which oils he considered a problem

According to Peat, the problem lies in soy, corn, safflower, canola, sesame, sunflower and palm oils, and in any oil labelled unsaturated or polyunsaturated. He added that almond oil, widely used in cosmetics, is also highly unsaturated 1.

He held that these oils are toxic whether they are cold-pressed or eaten inside the living plant, so it wasn't industrial processing that made them harmful. Since PUFA appear in lower concentrations in other parts of plants and in the animals that eat them, for him it is impossible to follow a diet completely free of them 1.

Enzymes, thyroid and immunity

Peat explained that unsaturated oils inhibit proteolytic enzymes, that is, the ones that break down proteins. According to him, those enzymes don't only serve digestion, but also the production of thyroid hormones, the clearing of clots and the support of immunity 13. That is why he thought someone could be malnourished even while eating well 1.

He pointed out that by around 1920 it was already known that PUFA inhibit those enzymes, and that producing unsaturated fat was then considered a trait of certain pathogens able to evade the immune system 4. He also suggested that plants make their own defensive toxins, and that seed oils serve that purpose 13.

Oxidation, ageing and tissue damage

Peat stressed that PUFA oxidise extremely easily. He told how a bottle of corn oil consumed oxygen almost as fast as his tissue samples in a respiration experiment 5. According to him, that oxidation triggers free radical reactions that can last for years in the body, because a fat cell takes a long time to renew itself 5.

He linked PUFA to the formation of lipofuscin, the so-called age pigment, and to the degeneration of the gonads and the brain. He considered that vitamin E only partly protects against these effects 6. He also saw ageing, especially of the brain, as increasingly associated with lipid peroxidation 3.

How they became popular, according to Peat

Peat held that seed oils stopped selling to the paint industry when petroleum-based paints arrived. It was then discovered that corn and soy fattened livestock on little food, and those products ended up being promoted for people 1. According to him, the focus on cholesterol was one more marketing tool of that industry 1.

He added that linoleic and linolenic acids were presented as essential and as a kind of drug against heart disease 2. For him, the term “essential” is inappropriate, because the body makes its own unsaturated fats when it doesn't eat them 1. He stated that evidence soon appeared that linoleic acid promotes heart disease, cancer and immune problems 2.

Why he distrusted fish oil

For Peat, fish fats are even more unstable than seed fats. Although, being long molecules, they inhibit the enzymes that metabolise fats less, they oxidise before reaching the blood 75. He held that their anti-inflammatory effect comes from those breakdown products, which in the long run weaken the immune system 78.

He also criticised that fish oil was declared safe without evaluating its antithyroid, immunosuppressive and peroxidative effects, among others 6. He compared that short-term relief with the anti-inflammatory X-ray treatments of the 1940s, whose harm took 10 to 15 years to show 8.

Fats he did consider suitable

Peat considered olive oil fairly safe because of its antioxidants, but advised not to go beyond a couple of teaspoons a day because of its PUFA content. He said toxicity also depends on total calories, because if the PUFA is burned as fuel straight away, the harm is relatively small 9.

He held that coconut oil and butter, with very little PUFA, can have an antioxidant effect and help clear the tissues of fats eaten earlier 9. He thought coconut oil prevents the inhibiting effect of unsaturated oils on metabolism 3, and that sugar, especially from fruit, is naturally converted into saturated fats 9.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Ray Peat say vegetable oils are toxic?
According to Peat, PUFA inhibit enzymes that break down proteins, which are needed for digestion, thyroid hormone and immunity 1. They also oxidise very easily and generate free radicals 5.
What did Peat think of olive oil?
He considered it fairly safe because of its variety of antioxidants, although it contains around 10% PUFA. That is why he advised no more than a couple of teaspoons a day 9.
What did Ray Peat think of fish oil and omega-3s?
He held that they are even more unstable than seed oils and that they oxidise before reaching the blood 7. For him, their anti-inflammatory effect comes from breakdown products that suppress immunity in the long run 78.
Are linoleic and linolenic acids essential according to Peat?
Peat considered the term “essential” inappropriate, because the body can synthesise its own unsaturated fats when they aren't in the diet 1. He held that their reputation as essential was promoted by a marketing campaign from the oil industry 2.
