Ray Peat on supplements

According to Peat, it is generally better to choose foods of high nutritional value than to turn to supplements, except in very special situations 1. He distrusted the purity of pills, capsules and powders, and held that many people reacted to the manufacturing ingredients 23. Even so, he accepted some specific supplements, and in his book he argued that it is unscientific to say they are unnecessary when people eat balanced meals 4.

Food first, supplements second

Peat thought multivitamins could be useful if someone found it very hard to get good food, but he warned that they are almost never well formulated and that even the best are made with somewhat contaminated chemicals 1. He therefore concluded that, except in very special cases, it is better to avoid them and to choose foods for their richness in nutrients 1.

He also said that he rarely recommended any supplement and that the best thing was to get nutrients from natural, pure foods 5. In one of his books he noted that if meals included liver, broccoli leaves, oysters and papaya, supplements would generally be unnecessary 6.

Allergies caused by excipients

Peat recounted that, decades ago, he counseled people with allergies in Eugene. At first he gave them supplements and a diet rich in vitamin A and pantothenic acid, and that helped a good share of them, but others did not improve 3. When he asked them to stop all supplements and eat natural foods, according to him they all improved, because many were allergic to the excipients in the products 37.

He also held that symptoms such as hay fever, chronic colds, asthma, diarrhea or headaches could be due to the ingredients used to make the pills 6. Even powdered vitamins and minerals, which tend to be purer, could contain allergens 6. He himself got migraines from certain vitamins, especially B2, ascorbic acid and rutin 1.

Contaminants and additives in the products

Peat explained that ascorbic acid was made from corn starch oxidized with industrial sulfuric acid, slightly contaminated with lead and other heavy metals 7. He added that tablets usually contain paraffin and talc as supposedly inert binders, and that zinc is hard to separate from cadmium, which is toxic 8.

For him, biological purification, such as that done by oysters, is usually more effective than industrial refining 8. Of the lecithin that is commonly sold, he said its unsaturated fatty acids oxidize in contact with air and go rancid, and he recommended eggs as a source of fresh lecithin 8.

Added iron and other isolated nutrients

According to Peat, the iron added to foods in the form of ferrous sulfate, cheap and reactive, had caused disease in animals by destroying the vitamins in the food 9. He therefore advised reading labels and avoiding, where possible, products with added iron 9. He also stated that iron destroys the vitamin E present in food, and he questioned giving pregnant women pills with doses far above the daily requirement without studying whether they raise the risk of miscarriage 10.

He was also of the opinion that amino acids such as tryptophan or glutamic acid should not be used as isolated supplements 2. And he asked that, if a supplement was needed, it not be used in excess, but only until the sufficient amount was found 2.

The cases where he did see a point

In one of his books, Peat cited a study of very well-nourished tuberculosis patients in which the group that received a supplement of vitamins, yeast and dried liver improved more than the placebo group 4. With it he argued that there are many similar studies and that it is unscientific to claim that supplements are unnecessary with balanced meals 4. He also acknowledged that individual peculiarities and stress can make it very hard to stay healthy on a normal diet 6.

He likewise noted that when antibiotics are used, the diet has to supply the nutrients made by the intestinal bacteria, such as vitamin K 211. On magnesium, he said it was reasonable if it did not cause headaches, asthma or diarrhea 5, and that pure, high-potency vitamin E seemed good to him 12. Of zinc he thought that doses above 10 milligrams a day are not safe 5.

Magnesium, thyroid and nutrient needs

For Peat, a higher energy metabolism reduces the need for almost any nutrient 13. He gave the example of magnesium: with thyroid and vitamin D at a good level, cells retain it and hardly need to replace it 13. By contrast, a person with low thyroid could become deficient in magnesium within a week even while taking large amounts 13.

In his books he described magnesium carbonate as useful for stabilizing nerves and muscles while the energy level rises, and he noted that many tablets contain far less magnesium than he considered reasonable 14. With vitamins K and E he pointed to another precaution: according to him, together they can form a charge-transfer complex that makes the body very sensitive to light for a few hours, and blue or ultraviolet light destroys riboflavin 15.

Frequently asked questions

Did Ray Peat recommend taking multivitamins?
According to Peat, they could only be useful if someone was in a very bad position to get good food 1. His conclusion was that, except in very special cases, it is better to avoid them and choose foods with a high nutrient content 1.
Why did Ray Peat say supplements could cause allergies?
He held that many people with persistent allergies were taking vitamin and mineral supplements every day and were in fact allergic to the excipients in the products 3. He recounted that, once they stopped, practically all of their local allergies cleared up 7.
What did Ray Peat think of magnesium and zinc supplements?
For him, magnesium was reasonable as long as it did not cause headaches, asthma or diarrhea, and he considered it very important 516. Of zinc he said the gluconate seemed acceptable, but that he did not believe doses above 10 milligrams a day were safe 5.
What did Ray Peat think of iron added to foods and pills?
According to Peat, the ferrous sulfate added to foods destroys vitamins and caused disease in animals, so he advised avoiding products with added iron 9. He added that iron destroys the vitamin E in food and asked for studies on whether inorganic iron raises the risk of miscarriage in pregnant women 10.
