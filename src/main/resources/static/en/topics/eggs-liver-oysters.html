Ray Peat on eggs, liver and oysters

For Ray Peat, eggs, liver and shellfish worked as a "shotgun" approach: three foods that cover deficiencies you do not even know you have 1. They supply above all vitamin A, B vitamins and minerals such as copper and zinc 23. Even so, he had qualifications about how to cook them and about the iron in some shellfish 45.

A combination to cover what we don't know

According to Peat, calculating nutrients one by one is too complicated. Sometimes a single supplement rescues someone in a serious state, but that kind of luck is rare 1. That is why he argued for the combination of eggs, liver and shellfish, which serves to fill in what is unknown 1.

The daily menu he proposes in a book addressed to women includes an egg at breakfast and an oyster stew at dinner 6.

Eggs and liver as sources of vitamin A

Peat held that one serving of liver a week, one or two eggs a day and milk are enough to cover vitamin A for most people 2. For him, any liver (fish, chicken or beef) is a great source, and so are eggs, milk and cheese 7.

He added that vitamin B12 is essential for turning carotene into vitamin A 7. He also explained that, together with thyroid hormone, vitamin A is the main cofactor for converting cholesterol into hormones 8. In his opinion, carotene accumulated in excess competes with the enzymes that use vitamin A and ends up acting as an anti-vitamin A 8.

How he preferred eggs, and cholesterol

Peat noted that something in raw yolk has a germicidal effect and helps disinfect the intestine. Cooking reduces that effect, but almost all of the benefit is kept 5. Even so, he recommended cooking eggs as little as possible, with the yolk runny, because its polyunsaturated fats (PUFA) oxidize and are easily damaged by heat 5.

On cholesterol, he cited studies in which adding eggs to the diet did not worsen the atherogenic character of LDL. One of them, with Mexican children who ate two eggs a day, found that the ratio of LDL to HDL was maintained 9. He also considered yolk a top-quality protein for refeeding malnourished people, sometimes together with thyroid hormone 10.

Liver: B vitamins, copper and other nutrients

Peat recommended eating liver once a week for its richness in B vitamins 1112. In another book he mentions having it at least twice a week 6. He also lists it as a food rich in folic acid, copper and ubiquinone 3.

He told the case of a man with a recurring infection to whom he gave liver and vitamins, and who by his account recovered in a few days 13. He explained that folic acid improves the function of white blood cells and that vitamin A is needed to synthesize proteins and form immune tissue 13. For him, persistent acne or dandruff suggested a lack of vitamin A 6.

Why liver can be hard to digest

Peat thought the difficulty with liver is mostly due to its low fat content. Its high level of protein sends strong signals to the body and calls for quite a lot of extra sugar, and preferably some saturated fat to slow absorption so that digestion is not stressful 1.

Oysters: copper, zinc and the iron problem

According to Peat, oysters are a source of zinc, a mineral he linked to immunity, wound healing, fertility and the nerves 3. He held that a lack of copper makes tissues retain too much iron, and so he advised regularly eating copper-rich foods such as shrimp and oysters 1415.

He valued copper because it allows oxygen to be used well to produce energy, keeps the skin and blood vessels elastic and takes part in hair color 14. On fish, he said he recommended it only for its reliable trace minerals, such as selenium and iodine, and he preferred shellfish and low-fat fish 4. He qualified this by noting that oysters have a lot of iron and that squid is a very good option, since it is low in iron and rich in copper 4.

Frequently asked questions

How much liver did Ray Peat recommend eating?
In his writing he speaks of one serving of liver a week, for its B vitamin content 11122. In another book he mentions having it at least twice a week 6. According to Peat, that weekly serving together with one or two eggs a day and milk covers vitamin A for most people 2.
Why did Ray Peat recommend oysters?
He considered them a source of zinc, and shellfish in general a source of copper 34. He held that a lack of copper leads tissues to accumulate iron, which for him was harmful 14. He also warned that oysters have a lot of iron, and pointed to squid as a low-iron alternative 4.
Did Ray Peat think eggs raise cholesterol?
He cited studies in which eating eggs did not worsen the atherogenic character of LDL 9. In one carried out with Mexican children, two eggs a day maintained the ratio of LDL to HDL 9. For Peat, moreover, vitamin A is needed, together with thyroid hormone, to convert cholesterol into hormones 8.
How did Ray Peat prefer to cook eggs?
He preferred to cook them as little as possible, with the yolk still runny 5. According to him, the polyunsaturated fats (PUFA) in the yolk oxidize and are easily damaged by heat, although a cooked egg keeps almost all of the benefit 5.
