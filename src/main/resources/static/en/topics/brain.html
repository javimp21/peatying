Ray Peat on the brain and intelligence

For Peat, the brain is above all an energy-producing system that depends on its oxidative metabolism 1. He held that brain size and intelligence are not limited by genes, but depend largely on environment, nutrition and stimulation 2.

The brain as an energy system

According to Peat, the brain is an energy-producing metabolic system like any other organ, but with a very intense oxidative metabolism and more dependent on oxygen than any other 1. That is why, when blood flow is cut off, consciousness stops almost instantly, even though ATP doesn't fall immediately 1. For him, consciousness is a metabolic process, and the computer metaphor contributes little to understanding the brain 1.

He also commented that the average brain weighs about 2% of the body but consumes between 40 and 50% of metabolic energy. From this he deduced that, in someone who spends a lot of mental energy or has a large brain, the metabolic difference can be enormous 3.

Glucose and progesterone in brain development

Peat often cited Zamenhof's experiments with chick embryos. The brain stopped growing when the glucose in the egg ran out, and when glucose was injected, growth continued until hatching 42. According to him, those chicks had larger brains, with more cells, and were more intelligent 54.

He added that the same researchers observed that progesterone increases brain size and corticosterone reduces it 5. And he pointed out that, in rats, treating the mothers with progesterone during gestation produced offspring with larger brains that learned more efficiently 2.

Genes, environment and stimulation

For Peat, Zamenhof's experiment showed that the chick's genetic nature was not a limit for its brain, and that brain complexity is realised thanks to the support of the environment 2. He also recalled that when rats were given an interesting environment, their brains grew and changed their enzyme activity. According to his account, the offspring of several generations showed a progressive increase in size and intelligence 42.

He criticised the idea that every trait is dictated by genes. He held that the foetus is built through interaction with the mother's physiology and that, even in adulthood, the environment shapes the development of brain cells 6. With normal stimulation and nutrition, he stated, even the adult brain can grow 1.

Stress, serotonin and brain damage

According to Peat, inappropriate stimulation is a stress that distorts growth, and stress mediators such as serotonin can cause persistent distortions of physiology and behaviour 7. He attributed to early stress learning problems, aggressive or compulsive behaviour, learned helplessness and shyness, among others 7.

He held that serotonin activates the glucocorticoid system, which can produce brain atrophy. In his opinion, protective neurosteroids such as pregnenolone and progesterone decrease with isolation, which leaves serotonin and glucocorticoids unopposed 7. He added that serotonin can reduce energy production by inhibiting mitochondrial respiratory enzymes and decrease the oxygen supply through vasoconstriction 8.

He also linked inescapable stress to nitric oxide, which puts the brain into demobilisation mode: depression, a desire to withdraw and, if withdrawal isn't allowed, anxiety and aggression 9.

Thyroid, steroids and brain protection

For Peat, keeping thyroid activity high is what matters most. According to him, when energy production falls, adrenaline, serotonin and estrogens rise, which can lead to mania or depression 10. He considered DHEA, progesterone and pregnenolone to be brain hormones 10, and that the memory improvement with pregnenolone or thyroid is due to curbing the stress hormones that dull the brain 7.

He believed the most physiological way to protect the brain is to keep the level of metabolic energy high. He explained that protective magnesium tends to be retained in proportion to the activity of thyroid hormone 11. He also attributed a protective effect to carbon dioxide, an indicator of good mitochondrial respiration, against the toxicity of estrogen on the nerves 11.

Education, language and mental development

According to Peat, education can activate or suppress mental energy: if it is mainly training in obedience, it suppresses it 7. He advocated a free education in which the curriculum isn't set by someone outside those who are studying 4. For him, learning and developing the brain are a single continuous process, not a genetic apparatus that is later filled with information 4.

He thought that if children were raised in conditions similar to the rats' interesting environment, their intelligence could be fostered 7. And he considered that language controlled by institutions, tied to a culture that seeks to manipulate others, can make us duller 4.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat say about glucose and the brain?
Peat stressed that in chick embryos the brain stopped growing when the glucose in the egg ran out. When glucose was injected, it kept growing until hatching and the chicks had larger brains 52. For him, this showed that the environment, and not only the genes, limits brain development 2.
Can the environment increase intelligence according to Ray Peat?
Yes. Peat cited experiments in which rats with a stimulating environment developed larger brains and, according to him, each generation showed a slight increase in size and intelligence 24. He held that the same should be explored with children 7.
Why did Ray Peat distrust serotonin in the brain?
According to Peat, serotonin is a stress substance that activates glucocorticoids, which can produce brain atrophy 7. He also held that it can reduce energy production by inhibiting mitochondrial enzymes 8.
What role did Ray Peat give the thyroid in brain function?
For him, keeping the thyroid active is what matters most, because when metabolism falls, adrenaline, serotonin and estrogen rise, which can cause mania or depression 10. He also linked it to the synthesis of pregnenolone and the retention of magnesium, both protective of the brain 711.
