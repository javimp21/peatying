Ray Peat on coffee

For Ray Peat, coffee is not just a harmful drug but a food with nutrients, and caffeine is a substance he described as something like a vitamin or an adaptogen 1. He held that popular ideas against coffee come from a health culture with mistaken notions, and that many doctors repeat them as if they were common sense 1. That is why he proposed reconsidering its place in diet and health care 1.

A food with vitamins and minerals

According to Peat, coffee provides very significant amounts of magnesium and other nutrients, such as vitamin B1 1. He recalled that dietetics textbooks usually say coffee has no nutritional value, but cited studies in a British nutrition journal according to which, in the UK diet, it provided around 20% of the intake of several essential vitamins and minerals 2.

He himself calculated that the coffee he drank each day gave him between 30 and 40 milligrams of niacin, and he considered it probably the most concentrated source of magnesium that can be obtained safely 2. That is why he believed that even decaffeinated coffee is a good supplement of some B vitamins and magnesium 2.

Protection against cancer and other diseases

What interested him most on the subject was a large monograph on caffeine, published around 1970, that described its protective effects against cancer 23. According to Peat, caffeine protected against tumours caused by radiation, chemicals, viruses and estrogens 21. He told of an experiment in which almost all mice exposed to concentrated tobacco smoke developed cancer, except in one week when the smoke was contaminated with caffeine and only 5% got sick. According to his account, when the experiment was repeated and other known carcinogens were tested, caffeine prevented them all 3.

He added other benefits attributed to coffee or caffeine: protection of the liver against alcohol, paracetamol and other toxins, and a lower incidence of thyroid disease among coffee drinkers 1. He also cited a lower frequency of breast cancer and Parkinson's disease 1. According to him, caffeine also curbs stress-induced cell death without disturbing the normal turnover of cells, and reduces the production of free radicals by inhibiting xanthine oxidase 1.

Mood, serotonin and energy

Peat pointed out that coffee drinkers have few suicides, and he linked this to serotonin 1. For him, excess serotonin produces features of depression, such as learned helplessness and a slower metabolism, while coffee stimulates the uptake of serotonin, increases metabolic energy and tends to improve mood 1. He added that in animals it reverses the state of hopelessness, often better than the so-called antidepressants 1.

He also held that caffeine improves the efficiency of cellular fuel use and performance 1. By stimulating brain metabolism it increases carbon dioxide, which dilates blood vessels 1. In the body as a whole, this lowers vascular resistance, favours circulation and eases the heart's work, although with a more active metabolism adequate nutrition is essential 1.

Coffee, progesterone and thyroid

According to Peat, caffeine closely resembles thyroid and progesterone, and drinking coffee or tea can help maintain their production or make up for a lack of them 1. He said that caffeine increases the concentration of progesterone in the blood and the brain, and that it acts in synergy with it 1.

That is why he interpreted women drinking more coffee before menstruation as spontaneous, rational self-medication, and criticised medical editors for reversing cause and effect and blaming coffee for symptoms it actually relieves 1. He added that some women notice more effect from a progesterone supplement if they take it with coffee, and that caffeine tends to activate thyroid secretion locally and reduce stress mediators 1.

Why he distrusted the studies against it

Peat stated that the negative effects attributed to coffee are usually obtained with large doses in a short time 1. On blood pressure, he held that caffeine's effect is slight and may not appear with normal consumption, and that experiments with coffee on an empty stomach confuse the effect of caffeine itself with that of falling glucose and rising adrenaline 1.

He reasoned similarly about pregnancy: in the experiments used to advise against coffee, enormous doses of caffeine delayed the growth of animal foetuses, but giving more sucrose or sugar prevented it 1. According to him, better-designed experiments might even show benefits for the foetus 1.

Coffee with meals, with cream and with iron

Peat explained that drinking coffee with a meal or after it has hardly any harmful effects 4. He told how some people who felt shattered and shaky after a single sip came to enjoy it within three or four days after adding thick cream and perhaps sugar, and drinking it only with meals 4. In a similar case, of women with symptoms of hypoglycaemia, he had suggested about 2 ounces (60 ml) of coffee with cream or milk with a meal, and the symptoms often decreased 1.

Another reason to drink it with meals is that, according to him, coffee inhibits iron absorption, which helps prevent iron overload 1. That is why he made a point of having it with meat 5. On butter in coffee, he thought each ingredient is good on its own, but that cream seemed better to him, especially for the taste 3.

Frequently asked questions

Did Ray Peat think coffee is good for your health?
Yes. He held that coffee provides nutrients and that caffeine has protective effects against several degenerative diseases, and he criticised the idea that it is just a harmful drug 12. He attributed many of the negative effects to large doses taken all at once or to poorly designed experiments 1.
What nutrients does coffee provide according to Ray Peat?
According to him, coffee provides magnesium, niacin and other B vitamins, such as B1 12. He considered even decaf a good supplement of some B vitamins and magnesium 2.
What did Ray Peat say about coffee and cancer?
Peat stated that caffeine protects against cancer caused by radiation, chemicals, viruses and estrogens 1. He based this on a monograph from around 1970 and on an experiment with mice exposed to tobacco smoke that he described in an interview 23.
How did Ray Peat suggest drinking coffee?
He said that, taken with or after meals, coffee has almost no harmful effects, and that people who tolerated it badly did well with cream and a little sugar, only with meals 4. Also, drinking it with food reduces iron absorption, something he valued 15.
