Ray Peat on progesterone

According to Peat, progesterone is not just the pregnancy hormone but the most protective hormone the body makes 12. For him it acts as the basic hormone of adaptation to stress and protects the brain and other tissues against excess estrogen 234.

Much more than a pregnancy hormone

Peat explained that progesterone's name, which refers to gestation, leads people to think it isn't needed when they aren't trying to get pregnant. For him that is a mistake, because it is the most protective hormone the body produces 12.

The large amounts made during pregnancy reflect, according to Peat, the baby's need to protect itself from a stressful environment 12. He added that the brain normally contains a very high concentration of progesterone, reflecting its protective function, and that the thymus, a key organ of the immune system, depends deeply on it 12.

Progesterone, stress and cortisol

According to Peat, experiments showed that progesterone is the basic hormone of adaptation and resistance to stress. The adrenal glands use it to make their anti-stress hormones, and with enough progesterone they don't need to produce cortisol, which he considered potentially harmful 2.

He held that if progesterone is lacking, too much cortisol is produced, and that excess cortisol causes osteoporosis, skin ageing, damage to brain cells and fat accumulation, especially on the back and abdomen 2. He attributed the progesterone deficit of menopause to a set of stress-related causes, including free radicals, prolactin, ACTH, estrogen, cortisol and a lack of thyroid, vitamin A or LDL cholesterol 5.

Protecting the brain and nerves

Peat pointed out that progesterone was being used to treat brain injuries with very good results, because it protects against inflammation and, in an early study, cut mortality by more than half compared with placebo 6. He also stated that it favours healing and regeneration after a brain injury and improves survival and recovery 3.

He linked this protection to its anticholinergic effect in the uterus. In rats poisoned with an organophosphate insecticide, progesterone prevented convulsions by reversing the inhibition of cholinesterase, and for him similar effects in menstruating women show that this is a general function of the hormone 6. He added that progesterone and pregnenolone protect brain cells against excitotoxins, so a chronically low level would accelerate the neuronal death induced by estrogen 5.

According to Peat, progesterone stabilises all nerves and is an important factor in restoring nerves and the brain. Together with thyroid and pregnenolone, it takes part in forming new myelin and in preventing the oedema that damages it 37.

Progesterone against estrogen and cancer

Peat cited the work of Alexander Lipschütz, who according to him showed around 1950 that estrogen causes fibrosis, fibroids and, over time, cancer, first in the uterus and then in the breasts, lungs, kidneys and brain. In all those cases, he said, progesterone was protective, and today some nine mechanisms by which it neutralises the estrogen problem would be known 4.

For him, the first thing estrogen does is make the cell take up water, which stimulates cell division, fat production and other enzymatic activities 4. He also recalled that in the 1930s experiments with primates and rodents had already shown the carcinogenic effect of estrogen and the protective effect of progesterone 8.

Peat described a study by the US National Cancer Institute with 17 women with cervical cancer, treated with daily injections of progesterone. On his reading, the photographs showed clear improvement, with softening of the tumour and an end to bleeding and pain, and the dose was no higher than what a woman produces in a healthy pregnancy 8.

Pregnancy, brain development and IQ

Peat told how Marion Diamond found in animals that estrogen during gestation slows brain growth, especially of the cortex, and that progesterone increases it. According to him, that gave a larger, more intelligent and less psychopathic brain 9.

He also drew on Katharina Dalton, who treated patients with premenstrual syndrome and menopause with progesterone. According to Peat, she found that the children of the treated women did better academically and had IQs of around 130, compared with about 96 for their unexposed older siblings, and this without her paying attention to thyroid, protein or salt 910.

Synthetic progestins and lack of promotion

Peat criticised the narrow definition of progestin, because it allows synthetic products to be sold as progestogens, some of them strongly estrogenic and used as contraceptives. He found it strange that agents which suppress progesterone synthesis are presented as favourable to pregnancy 11. Of medroxyprogesterone, he said that it reduces the ovary's production of progesterone, while natural progesterone stimulates its synthesis 11.

He held that the estrogen industry had convinced most doctors that progesterone didn't work orally and was inferior to their patented drugs. That is why, according to him, it was given mostly as injections with toxic solvents that cause inflammation 8. In an interview he stated that, in terms of raw material, estrogen is far cheaper than progesterone, and that it all comes down to money 9.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Ray Peat say progesterone is protective?
For Peat it was the body's most protective hormone and the basis of adaptation to stress, because it lets the adrenals make anti-stress hormones without resorting to cortisol 12. He added that the brain contains it in high concentration and that it protects the nerves and the brain 13.
What relationship did Peat see between progesterone and estrogen?
He held that the two hormones act in opposite directions: estrogen promotes fibrosis, fibroids and cancer, and progesterone protects against all of them 4. He also said that estrogen slows brain growth during gestation and progesterone stimulates it 9.
What did Peat think about progesterone and the brain?
According to Peat, progesterone protects against inflammation and favours regeneration after a brain injury 63. He also said that it protects brain cells against excitotoxins and takes part in forming myelin 57.
Was Peat in favour of synthetic progestins?
No. Peat pointed out that medroxyprogesterone acetate reduces the ovary's production of progesterone, while natural progesterone stimulates its synthesis 11. He criticised calling synthetics progestogens when they are sometimes very estrogenic and used as contraceptives 11.
