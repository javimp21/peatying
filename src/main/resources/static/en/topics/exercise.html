Ray Peat on exercise

For Ray Peat, exercise is useful when it stimulates muscle without setting off stress. He held that well-dosed effort supports the protective hormones and the health of the mitochondria, while too much raises cortisol and pushes the body toward lactic acid metabolism 12. That is why he cared more about the type of exercise than about the amount 2.

Protective steroids and tolerance for effort

According to Peat, pregnenolone, progesterone, DHEA and testosterone form a first barrier against damage, with overlapping protective effects that keep the energy-producing and sensory systems stable 3. With plenty of these hormones, he said, an effort such as running a marathon or swimming in icy water would not be harmful 3.

When they are lacking, he held that anything becomes stressful, even walking or breathing harder, and the body shifts to lactic acid metabolism and emergency cortisol production 3. In his view, aging or malnutrition can reduce that protective production 3.

Concentric versus eccentric exercise

Peat distinguished concentric contraction, in which the muscle shortens against resistance, from eccentric contraction, in which it lengthens while bearing a load. According to him, the first improves muscle function without injuring it, and the second damages the muscle 4. His example was walking up a mountain, which is concentric, versus walking down it, which is eccentric and causes soreness 45.

He explained that eccentric exercise uses less oxygen, produces less carbon dioxide, raises lactic acid and oxidizes more fat. He saw a similar pattern in prolonged stress 6. On gym machines, the concentric part was the one that, in his view, did not destroy muscle fibers as much 5.

Mitochondria and concentric exercise

Peat cited studies by exercise physiologists on older people whose muscle mitochondria were badly deteriorated. After a few weeks of concentric-only exercise, as he told it, those people had new, functional mitochondria, and their mitochondrial DNA had returned to normal 452.

Since for him the mitochondrion is the source of steroid production, looking after it was the reason to prefer that kind of effort 2. His examples were running up the stairs and sliding down the banister, or cycling up a hill and coasting down 2. For weights, he mentioned machines that let you lift the load and release it without braking it 2.

Cortisol, androgens and muscle mass

According to Peat, a well-stimulated muscle produces testosterone and other androgens, while a muscle under too much stress produces cortisol 1. He recounted that in the 1960s some German trainers managed to strengthen muscle with moderate resistance, without raising cortisol. Their athletes had androgens so high that they were accused of doping 1.

He also noted that, according to the Russians, combining exercise with more protein can increase muscle mass 7. But he warned that if exercise produces a lot of stress and little muscular action, cortisol diverts amino acids into making glucose and the muscle wastes away 7. For him, sustained cortisol also affects the skin, the immune system and the brain 7.

Lactic acid, blood sugar and thyroid

Peat explained that once you pass the threshold at which lactic acid rises, the feeling of breathlessness appears. According to him, lactate has a pro-inflammatory effect and goes together with a fall in blood sugar, because producing it is far less efficient than aerobic oxidation 2. With low blood sugar, he said, the active thyroid hormone cannot be formed 2. In a very healthy person, the liver clears the lactate during rest. With poor nutrition, a single bout of exercise with lactate could be enough to lower the metabolism 2.

He held that the lower the thyroid function, the lower the threshold for switching to lactate metabolism. With high thyroid function, the body would work as it does at high altitude, where T3 rises and more work can be done without producing lactic acid 8. He also thought that extreme fatigue and exhausting exercise can raise ammonia in the blood, something he associated with an excess of lactic acid 9.

Intense training, pulse and sexuality

In one of his books, Peat noted that athletic training lowers the pulse. According to him, the cortisol produced by stress inhibits the thyroid, and that can be a useful adaptation for endurance, because with low thyroid less oxygen is needed 10. He added that these hormonal changes are known to produce sterility in men and women 10.

His conjecture was that intense exercise, such as running, reduces sexual responsiveness just as it reduces fertility. For him, emotional stress or nutritional deficiencies could produce the same effect 10. In that same book he considered aerobic exercise the most stressful kind, because lactate production is the main signal that new glucose has to be made 7.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of exercise did Ray Peat recommend?
According to Peat, the ideal was mostly concentric exercise, with a load while the muscle shortens and no load while it relaxes and lengthens 2. His examples were running up the stairs or cycling uphill and coming down without effort 2. For him, this protects the muscles and the mitochondria 52.
Why did Ray Peat distrust intense aerobic exercise?
For Peat, getting out of breath indicates lactic acid production, and that is the signal that the body needs to make new glucose, which he linked to cortisol and to muscle wasting 7. He also held that lactate goes together with a drop in blood sugar and a brake on the formation of active thyroid hormone 2.
What link did Ray Peat see between exercise and testosterone?
According to Peat, a properly stimulated muscle produces androgens such as testosterone, but a stressed muscle produces cortisol 1. That is why he argued for exercising in a way that keeps cortisol low and the androgens active 1.
What did Ray Peat say about muscle soreness and eccentric exercise?
Peat explained that eccentric exercise, such as walking down a mountain, stretches the muscle while it bears a load and damages it, which causes soreness 45. He added that it uses less oxygen, produces less carbon dioxide and raises lactic acid 6.
