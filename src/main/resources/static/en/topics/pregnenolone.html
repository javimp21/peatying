Ray Peat on pregnenolone

For Ray Peat, pregnenolone is a protective hormone that the body makes from cholesterol and from which progesterone and DHEA are derived 1. He held that in a healthy person it has hardly any effect, but that under stress it restrains excess hormones such as cortisol 23. According to him, its production falls sharply with age, which would help explain much of the decline of ageing 4.

Where pregnenolone comes from

According to Peat, pregnenolone is formed when a chain is cut off cholesterol. This mainly requires thyroid hormone, which stimulates oxidation, and vitamin A, which activates the enzymes involved 1. So, if cholesterol, thyroid or vitamin A are lacking, he argued there will necessarily be a pregnenolone deficiency 1.

He explained that pregnenolone is the immediate precursor of progesterone and DHEA. DHEA in turn gives rise to androgens and estrogens 1. With few precursors, the body would keep making the end hormones, which at the extreme are cortisol and estrogen. The result would be chronic exposure to high cortisol and estrogen 1.

Pregnenolone, stress and cortisol

One feature Peat emphasised is that, taken orally, it does nothing noticeable in a healthy animal or person. If stress hormones are high, however, they return to normal 3. He recounted that in old experiments with stressed animals, a huge dose of pregnenolone left them more normal than before they received it 5.

For him, it also restrains the stress process: it reduces the activity of ACTH and the adrenal hormones and blocks their effects 1. He added that, by normalising stress hormones, thyroid function, which in his view stress suppresses, often seems to be corrected 3. He also held that it helps protect nerves against excitotoxins, which during stress and fatigue activate neurons to the point of cumulative damage 3.

The brain and ageing

Peat pointed out that pregnenolone is one of the brain's main hormones, which the brain partly produces and also takes up from the blood 2. The brain contains much more pregnenolone, DHEA and progesterone than other organs or the blood, and those levels fall over the years 3. In old age only about 5% of what is produced in youth would be produced 4.

According to him, other hormones such as cortisone decline much less, so the balance shifts towards those that use up body substance without rebuilding it 4. Even if average cortisol stays the same, in practice it becomes more active and the person is more sensitive to its side effects 6. He mentioned that in ageing rats pregnenolone improved memory and performance, and that human studies since the 1940s showed improvements in everyday tasks 2. He also recalled that in 1944 some factory workers performed better during the weeks they took it 7.

Skin and the face-lift effect

Peat considered the skin one of the most important endocrine organs, able to metabolise steroids, and suspected pregnenolone matters to it as much as to the brain 8. He cited a 1965 study in which pregnenolone or progesterone applied to the skin restored youthful properties to aged skin 7.

He recounted that his own facial skin, which had sagged over the years, tightened soon after he started taking a small amount. He said he had seen the same in other people 8. He attributed the effect to better circulation and to the contraction of muscle-like skin cells 48. According to him, a similar effect might improve joint mobility, lung elasticity and even eyesight 4.

Safety and his personal experience with dose

Peat considered pregnenolone the safest of pregnenolone, DHEA and progesterone. He warned that an excess of androgen, such as DHEA, can raise estrogen 9. He said that for a year he took an average of 3,000 or 4,000 mg a day, when the full replacement dose is about 30 mg, without side effects and with great resistance to stress 9. He recalled that in rats huge doses did no harm, other than stopping the production of stress hormones 10.

One caveat he mentioned is the purity of the product: one part per thousand of estrogen in a pregnenolone preparation could have potent effects, so it was worth looking for small peaks of estrogen or other steroids in the analyses 11.

Why it was never developed as a drug

According to Peat, in the 1950s there were already studies showing it was better than cortisone or cortisol as an anti-inflammatory 10. Unlike other hormones, which reduce the body's own production, progesterone and pregnenolone support it. So he thought one or two doses can restart normal production if there is enough thyroid, vitamin A and cholesterol 10.

He held that it stabilises damaged mitochondria and lets cells organise energy production. He attributed the drug companies' lack of interest to the fact that these hormones tend to cure diseases 10. He also wrote that an oral dose keeps acting for days and partly improves the body's ability to make its own pregnenolone 4.

What is pregnenolone according to Ray Peat?
For Peat it is a hormone formed from cholesterol with the help of thyroid and vitamin A, and from which progesterone and DHEA are derived 1. He saw it as a protective hormone, abundant in youth and present in the brain and skin 24.
Why did Peat say pregnenolone reduces stress?
He held that it lowers the activity of ACTH and the adrenal hormones and blocks their effects 1. According to him, if cortisol is high, pregnenolone brings it back to normal 23. A healthy, unstressed person would notice nothing 2.
What did Peat think of pregnenolone's effect on the skin?

Did Peat consider pregnenolone safe?
