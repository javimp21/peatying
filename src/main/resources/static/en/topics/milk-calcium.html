Ray Peat on milk and calcium

According to Peat, milk and cheese are useful foods for an adult, above all because of their calcium, which helps keep parathyroid hormone (PTH) low 12. For him, what does harm is excess phosphate relative to calcium, not drinking milk, and milk has a favourable ratio between the two 34.

Why the calcium in milk matters

Peat explained that if the diet doesn't provide enough calcium, blood calcium falls and PTH rises, pulling calcium out of the bones to compensate. According to him, that hormone also contributes to inflammation, to the calcification of soft tissues and to lower respiratory energy production 1.

He also linked a higher calcium intake with a leaner build. He drew this from his observations travelling in Europe and Mexico, and held that studies in which regular milk drinkers gain less weight point to calcium as the main factor, although good-quality protein and saturated fat also play a part 1.

Excess phosphate and calcification

For Peat, excess phosphate pushes calcium out of the bones and into the arteries. He explained that disproportionate phosphate activates the parathyroid glands, and that PTH mobilises bone calcium by blocking oxidative metabolism and generating local lactic acid 3.

According to his explanation, if that activation is chronic, the soft tissue runs out of energy. Crystals of calcium phosphate form in the mitochondria and, with the lactic acid, those crystals don't dissolve 3. That is why he advocated a diet with enough calcium to curb parathyroid activity and low in phosphate 2.

The calcium-phosphorus ratio of milk

Peat pointed out that the foods with the most phosphate relative to calcium are grains, legumes, meats and fish, and that many prepared foods contain added phosphate. Green leaves, many fruits, milk and cheese have a safer ratio 2. According to him, phosphate from milk and cheese is associated in epidemiological studies with lower blood pressure 2.

For him, milk's ratio of 1.3 to 1 is very close to a safe ratio, although he believed two or three times more phosphate than calcium can be tolerated if sugar intake is good. He explained that fructose helps shed phosphate faster, and that some Americans take in seven times more phosphate than calcium 4.

How milk combines with other foods

Peat held that combining milk and cheese with fruit strengthens their anti-stress effect. The extra sugar and potassium make better use of milk protein, moderate cortisol and help curb PTH 1.

He also said that spreading a food over smaller meals can lower PTH, and that milk is convenient to take in small amounts and often 1. He added that lactose and fat slow the absorption of amino acids, which reduces cortisol, and that casein seems to have direct anti-stress effects 1.

Lactose intolerance, according to Peat

For Peat, the idea that certain ethnic groups can't digest lactose because of their genes was a myth. He cited tests in which, when milk with and without lactose was compared, intolerant people noticed no difference, and others in which, after a few weeks of drinking milk every day, they adapted 1. He held that half a glass or a quarter of a glass a day for a few weeks is enough to induce the enzyme again 56.

He also attributed intolerance to low thyroid function, which reduces digestive enzymes. He added that a lack of progesterone or thyroid, or a bacterial infection in the small intestine, makes the enzyme disappear, and that bacteria then ferment the lactose and produce gas 56.

Milk quality and allergies

According to Peat, milk from well-fed cows contains proteins similar to those of leaves, and the bacteria in the rumen break down a good part of the toxins and allergens. On very poor diets, for example with a lot of grain, the filtering is less perfect and some allergen may reach the milk 1.

For him, the cow's environment affects the milk, and mixing the milk of thousands of cows gives a standardised product, while in a small dairy what the cow ate the day before makes a difference 6. He pointed out that lower-fat milk has vitamins A and D added by law, and that the carrier for those vitamins and its contaminants can cause allergies, so whole milk would be the most likely to be allergen-free 17. He also cited carrageenan as an allergen that, in his view, disturbs calcium metabolism 1.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Ray Peat recommend milk?
According to Peat, milk provides calcium, good-quality protein, sugar and fat, and its calcium helps restrain parathyroid hormone 1. Also, for an adult its low iron content can be useful to avoid iron overload 1.
Does the calcium in milk end up in the arteries according to Peat?
For Peat it isn't the calcium in milk but excess phosphate that pushes calcium into the arteries and out of the bones, through parathyroid hormone 3. He held that milk has a ratio of calcium to phosphate close to a safe ratio 4.
What did Peat think of lactose intolerance?
He held that it isn't a fixed genetic matter: for him it depends on exposure to milk and on factors such as thyroid function 56. He said that taking small amounts daily for a few weeks lets you regain the enzyme that digests lactose 56.
Is whole or skimmed milk better according to Peat?
Peat considered whole milk the most likely to be allergen-free, because lower-fat milk has added vitamins A and D, with a carrier and possible contaminants that can cause problems 1. He added that different brands of milk can agree with people very differently 71.
