The Ray Peat diet

According to Peat, the healthiest diet is based on plenty of protein, few unsaturated fats, little iron and lots of antioxidants, with low or moderate starch intake 1. In practice, for him this means relying on milk, cheese, eggs, shellfish, fruit and coconut oil 1. He also held that polyunsaturated vegetable oils are one of the big problems of modern eating 2.

The foods at the core of his diet

Peat proposed that a large part of the diet be milk, cheese, eggs, shellfish, fruit and coconut oil, with vitamin E and salt as the safest supplements 1. He considered that this pattern served as an anti-ageing diet and also to prevent and treat diabetes and connective tissue diseases 1.

He warned that amino acids, especially those in eggs, stimulate insulin secretion. According to him, this can cause hypoglycaemia and, with it, the release of cortisol 1. He thought that having fruit or another carbohydrate, coconut oil and salt in the same meal reduces that effect of protein 1.

Milk and cheese for weight loss

To lose weight, Peat described first a period without eating and then a diet of low-fat milk and cheese, with some orange juice and occasionally eggs and oysters 3. He held that milk and cheese as the base greatly increase heat production, energy metabolism and oxygen consumption, which makes losing weight easier 3.

In his opinion, calcium and vitamin D support thyroid hormone, and their deficiency is very common and slows metabolism 3. He added that a high intake of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) impairs both vitamin D metabolism and that of the thyroid 3. He also wanted to lower dietary phosphorus relative to calcium 3.

Fats: why he avoided seed oils

Peat summed up that unsaturated fats cause ageing, clotting, inflammation, cancer and weight gain, and recommended avoiding corn, soy, safflower, flax, cottonseed, rapeseed, peanut and sesame oils 2. He told how the seed oil industry lost its market in paints and varnishes and how its crops went to fattening animals and then to being promoted for people 2. According to him, the talk about cholesterol was just a marketing tool of that industry 2.

In his list of practical guidelines, he advised using coconut oil, butter and olive oil, as well as vitamin E 2. He said that lard is usually as toxic as those oils, because pigs are fed corn and soy, and that lean meat is better 2. He considered dried fish and shellfish once or twice a week good, and advised against cod liver oil 2. He also stated that unsaturated fats intensify the harmful effects of estrogens 2.

Sugar and fruit versus starch

Peat gave great importance to a 1938 experiment in which a man lived for six months on sucrose syrup, milk, cottage cheese and orange juice 4. The researchers observed that his usual tiredness disappeared, that his high blood pressure and high cholesterol returned to normal and that his lifelong migraines stopped 45. For Peat, the most interesting thing was that his blood phosphate fell 4.

He interpreted those results as a sign that polyunsaturated fats lower metabolism and hinder the use of sugars, so the normal diet would be diabetogenic 5. Even so, he believed sugars are healthier in the long run when they come with minerals, as in milk and fruit 6. He preferred fruit to grains because, calorie for calorie, starch is more fattening than sucrose and also feeds gut bacteria 7.

Grains, legumes and starches

Peat recommended avoiding grain and legume starches, and using fruit as a main part of the diet to minimise the effects of polyunsaturated fats 6. He linked coeliac disease and gluten sensitivity with diabetes and hypothyroidism, and added that gluten cross-reacts with an enzyme expressed under the influence of estrogens 6. According to him, the complex starches of unsprouted or unfermented legumes and grains are hard to digest and can cause gas and intestinal inflammation 7.

He also cited the work of an immunologist according to which large starch granules can pass into the blood, block capillaries and expose the immune system to antigens 8. If starch is eaten, he suggested cooking it very well, for 40 to 60 minutes, and eating it with fat, which slows its absorption and lets the enzymes soften it 8.

Protein, gelatin and plant toxins

Peat cited a National Academy of Sciences study for the army indicating that even adults with office jobs need at least 100 grams of protein a day 9. He pointed out that the highest-quality proteins are rich in methionine, cysteine and tryptophan, which can be toxic in excess and cause convulsions or inflammation 9. That is why he preferred gelatin, which lacks those amino acids, and considered that milk provides anti-stress factors, above all its calcium 9.

He thought that a lack of protein lowers body temperature, slows the thyroid and promotes inflammation and clotting 10. On plants, he held that seeds contain toxins and that their protein has little value, while fruits, roots and tubers provide many nutrients with few antimetabolic substances 11. For him, studies on protein requirements were done for the agricultural industry and aimed at the fastest, cheapest growth 12.

What foods did Ray Peat recommend?

Why did Ray Peat reject polyunsaturated vegetable oils?
He held that unsaturated fats cause ageing, clotting, inflammation, cancer and weight gain 2. He also believed they lower metabolism and that a normal diet containing them promotes diabetes 5.
What did Peat think of grains and starch?
For him, starch amounts to pure glucose and, calorie for calorie, is more fattening than fruit sugar, as well as feeding gut bacteria 7. He recommended avoiding grains and legumes, and if starch is eaten, cooking it well and eating it with fat 68.
How much protein did Ray Peat advocate?
