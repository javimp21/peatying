Ray Peat on protein and meat

Ray Peat was not against protein: he held that an adult needs a good amount every day 1. What worried him was muscle meat, rich in tryptophan, cysteine and methionine, amino acids that he believed slow the thyroid and feed stress 23. So he preferred to spread protein across gelatin, dairy and other foods, rather than eat only muscle 21.

Why he distrusted muscle meat

For Peat, eating only lean meat delivers to the blood the same mix of amino acids produced under intense stress, when excess cortisol breaks down the body's own muscle for energy 2. He explained that muscle is very rich in tryptophan and cysteine, and that both slow thyroid function 4.

According to him, tryptophan is converted into serotonin, serotonin stimulates more cortisol, and cortisol intensifies muscle breakdown, which releases more tryptophan. In his view this forms a circle that also depresses the thyroid 25.

Gelatin as his preferred protein

Peat pointed out that gelatin contains no tryptophan and only small amounts of cysteine, methionine and histidine 2. So he saw it as a simple way to restrict the amino acids that, in his judgement, are associated with the problems of ageing 2. He also cited animal experiments in which removing almost all methionine extended life by 30 to 40 per cent 6.

He also advocated eating the whole animal, with fish broths, chicken feet or ox cheek, because that provides plenty of glycine and gelatin, which for him gives a healthier amino acid balance than muscle alone 2. He noted that people who eat the tough parts of the animal get more gelatin than those who eat tender steaks 6. Asked about his favourite protein, he confirmed it was gelatin 1.

How much protein an adult needs

Peat cited a US National Academy of Sciences study for the army, according to which even average-sized women and men doing office work needed at least about 100 grams of protein a day 1. On other occasions he spoke of 75 to 100 grams a day 7. In a supplementation programme for times of stress he proposed about 90 grams, with liver, eggs, milk and shellfish 8.

He added that a significant part of that amount could be gelatin for adults, and that for a growing child a large dose of refined gelatin would displace other necessary foods 2. In his opinion, the requirements for tryptophan and cysteine are very low in adulthood 2, although not zero: they are needed to renew tissues such as hair, skin and the cells of the intestine 3.

Age, activity and protein

According to Peat, a growing young person with a very high metabolism needs more protein rich in methionine, cysteine and tryptophan, and that proportion changes with age and activity level 3. He said metabolism drops quickly after puberty and then declines steadily every year, so the older a person is, the more careful they should be with an excess of excitotoxic amino acids 7.

He recounted that Broda Barnes, when he ate much more meat, had to double his thyroid dose. Peat interpreted this as the effect of displacing carbohydrate and releasing amino acids that, in his view, suppress the thyroid 3.

Why pair protein with sugar

Peat held that a meal rich in protein and without sugar or other carbohydrate triggers insulin, which lowers blood glucose. That drop, according to him, pushes cortisol up and causes a large rise in this hormone 95. He attributed to this the fact that some people look diabetic or very hyperglycemic from eating too much meat without carbohydrate 9.

For him, the practical consequence was to keep a balance between protein and sugar and always eat sugar together with protein 9. He added that sugar restrains cortisol, which spares protein, and that it also inhibits the chronic release of fatty acids from body stores 10.

Milk and fruit versus meat

Peat observed that the highest-quality proteins tend to be those richest in methionine, cysteine and tryptophan, which he considered potentially toxic in excess, with a risk of seizures or inflammation 1. According to him, milk provides anti-stress factors that offset the effect of those amino acids, its high calcium content being the most important 1.

In an interview, the host recalled that Peat recommended getting most protein from dairy and limiting eggs to two a day, and Peat replied that, as an adult, it is best to follow one's own biological urges rather than advertising 7.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat think of eating a lot of meat?
According to Peat, it is easy to overdo protein, especially if it displaces carbohydrate, because tryptophan, cysteine and methionine are released, which for him slow the thyroid 3. He held that muscle meat alone reproduces the amino acid balance of extreme stress 2.
Why did Ray Peat prefer gelatin to meat?
Because, according to him, gelatin lacks tryptophan and has little cysteine, methionine and histidine, amino acids he linked to inflammation and ageing 2. He also said high doses of protein are better tolerated when taken as natural gelatin 3.
How much protein a day did Ray Peat recommend?
He cited a National Academy of Sciences study that set a minimum of about 100 grams a day to work efficiently 1, and in another interview 75 to 100 grams was mentioned 7. He thought much of it could come from gelatin for adults 2.
Why did Peat say you should eat sugar with protein?
He explained that protein without carbohydrate stimulates insulin, lowers glucose and raises cortisol 9. According to him, sugar restrains cortisol and spares protein 10.
