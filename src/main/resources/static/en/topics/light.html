Ray Peat on light and the sun

For Ray Peat, light is a first-order biological factor: red light activates energy production in cells, and the sun is beneficial as long as you avoid sunburn 12. He also held that prolonged darkness is a source of stress that damages the mitochondria and speeds up ageing 34.

Red light and cellular energy

According to Peat, red and orange light penetrate tissues well because water absorbs little of it. So it can act on cellular pigments such as cytochrome oxidase, which it activates, increasing ATP production. For him, this counteracts the toxic effects of ultraviolet 1.

He also held that red light favours the formation of new cells and mitochondria, speeds healing and reduces scar fibrosis 4. He added that its red component calms electrons excited by inflammation or by metabolic inhibitors such as fatty acids 5. He criticised established science for treating visible light as chemically inactive, when as early as the 1960s there were studies showing that blue light inhibits respiratory enzymes and red light activates them 6.

The sun: a benefit if there is no burn

Peat explained that ultraviolet only reaches the skin and can damage a key enzyme there, but that the sun's red light restores it. That is why, in his opinion, the sun is a net benefit if you avoid burning 2. He considered avoiding sunburn the only important precaution 7.

For him, many hours a day of bright light, whether sunlight or incandescent bulbs, help balance hormones and raise the defensive ones, such as progesterone and testosterone 7. He also mentioned an experiment in which red light on rats' heads improved their learning 7.

Ultraviolet, sunburn and vitamin D

According to Peat, excess ultraviolet produces damage similar to that of ionising radiation and generates systemic inflammation. He gave the example that a severe burn on one arm is felt throughout the body 8. He held that stored vitamin D is a protective anti-inflammatory that curbs that inflammation 8.

He explained that ultraviolet is needed to synthesise vitamin D, while red light neutralises the free radicals produced by stress. So, although early-morning sun doesn't produce vitamin D, for him it does provide the anti-stress effect 9. He added that with enough calcium and other nutrients one can get by with very little vitamin D, and he recalled animal experiments where sugar, unlike starch, allowed strong bones to form without that vitamin 9.

Darkness as stress for the body

Peat cited studies in which cortisol began to rise a few minutes after the lights went out and kept increasing during the night. Sleeping slowed that rise, which is why he considered deep sleep the best defence 35. According to him, the stress system immediately recognises darkness as a form of stress 3.

He held that in darkness cytochrome oxidase breaks down and that, after about 12 to 15 hours, many mitochondria swell and some burst, which for him is part of ageing 3. He added that prolonged darkness raises cortisol, which slows the proliferation of stem cells 4. Other stress hormones, such as adrenaline and prolactin, also rise at night, while thyroid and progesterone fall 10.

Light, darkness and the sensitivity of the eyes

According to Peat, night-time and stress hormones, especially melatonin and prolactin, increase the eyes' sensitivity to light. Eyes adapted to darkness can suffer serious damage from light that would normally be harmless 6.

For him, the problem comes from too much darkness, since the eyes adapt to excess light but darkness increases their sensitivity 6. He held that the retina is damaged when it contains too many polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA), because they react with light and free radicals, especially when there is stress and excess iron 6.

Incandescent bulbs and ordered water

Peat stated, based on animal experiments, that the protective enzyme can be damaged by just eight hours of darkness. He believed small fluorescent bulbs would cause an epidemic of cancer and that incandescent bulbs only protect if they are several hundred watts 2.

According to him, work at the University of Ulm showed that 670 nm red light stabilises a more ordered state of the hydrogen bonds in water, which would help explain its protective and restorative effects. He thought this reordering could be valuable in cataracts, together with red light's restorative effect on ATP 1.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat think about sunbathing?
For Peat, the sun was beneficial in general and his only precaution was to avoid sunburn 7. He held that, if there is no burn, the sun is a net benefit because red light restores the enzyme that ultraviolet damages in the skin 2.
Why did Peat say red light is beneficial?
According to Peat, red light penetrates tissues and activates cytochrome oxidase, which increases ATP production 1. He also stated that it favours the formation of mitochondria, healing and cell renewal 4.
Why did Peat consider darkness a stress?
He held that cortisol starts to rise a few minutes after the lights go out and that prolonged darkness breaks down cytochrome oxidase and damages the mitochondria 3. That is why he saw sleep as the main defence against that stress 5.
What did Peat think of fluorescent bulbs?
Peat believed that small fluorescent bulbs would cause an epidemic of cancer. According to him, only incandescent bulbs of several hundred watts provide the protective red light 2.
