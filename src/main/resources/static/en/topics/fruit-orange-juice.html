Ray Peat on fruit and orange juice

According to Peat, ripe fruit and orange juice are a good source of sugars that come with minerals, especially when taken with proteins such as egg or milk 12. For him, the fear of the juice's glycemic index was inconsistent, because the starches recommended in its place raise blood sugar more 3. He also insisted that fruit should be fully ripe and of good quality 45.

Orange juice and the glycemic index

Peat pointed out that orange juice and sucrose have a lower glycemic index than starch, whole wheat bread or white bread. Even so, he said, dietitians commonly advise against juice because its index is similar to that of a cola drink 3. For him, if the glycemic index mattered so much, the consistent thing would be to recommend the cola or the juice in place of white bread 3.

He recalled that in the 1970s people began to be told to eat complex carbohydrates, with whole grains and legumes, and to avoid fruit juices 3. According to him, after decades of promoting starchy foods, obesity and deaths from diabetes had increased 3. He attributed the possible harm of starch not so much to the sustained glucose that the index measures as to the stress reaction that follows a strong release of insulin 3.

Fruit and protein: why combine them

For Peat, the sugars and minerals in fruit help stabilize blood sugar and allow protein to be used for building tissue instead of being burned as energy. According to him, that combination lowers the stress hormones and supports thyroid function 1. His examples were a glass of orange juice with an egg, or cherries with cheese 1.

He explained that amino acids stimulate the release of insulin and that, without sugar in the meal, glucose falls. The body then responds with adrenaline or cortisol, and cortisol turns part of the protein into sugar and fat 63. That is why he held that handling protein well requires glucose or fructose to keep blood sugar steady 6.

Minerals, potassium and calcium in juice and milk

Peat compared orange juice with rice, pasta or bread, which according to him are poor in the minerals that fruit supplies in abundance. He said that potassium, plentiful in all fruits, acts in a way similar to insulin, so that less insulin has to be secreted to handle the same amount of glucose 2. He added that the juice contains other substances, such as naringin, that would help prevent inflammation 2.

In his opinion, milk completes the picture: its protein and fat slow the absorption of lactose, and its calcium strongly stimulates energy metabolism, so that the sugar is used before it is stored as fat. That is why he thought milk drinkers tend to be leaner 2. He saw in milk the same principle of combining sugar, protein and minerals 1.

Fructose, oxidative stress and refined sugar

Peat cited a study in which people who drank a glucose beverage showed a large increase in oxidative and inflammatory stress, while that increase did not appear with fructose or with orange juice 7. He also mentioned that a derivative of fructose, fructose diphosphate, has protective effects against inflammation and against injury from lack of oxygen 7.

According to him, a daily diet with about two quarts of milk and one of orange juice supplies enough fructose and other sugars to resist stress in general. Larger amounts of juice or honey could protect against greater stress and even reverse some degenerative conditions 7. Of refined sugar he said that it lacks essential nutrients and should be seen only as a temporary or occasional resource when good fruit is not available 7. He also warned that replacing foods such as fruit, milk or eggs with sugar can lead to deficiencies 6.

Ripeness and quality of fruit and juice

Peat stated that many supermarket fruits are not properly ripe and are hard to digest. According to him, when the polymers in the fruit have not broken down they feed bacteria and not the person, and gas is the sign that something is wrong 4. He thought that an ordinary apple contains a lot of pectin and little sugar, and that eating it largely feeds the bacteria. The same would apply to hard pears and underripe peaches 89.

For juice he recommended fully ripe oranges or tangerines grown without chemicals, and judging by the taste 5. He also criticized large companies for using enzymes to break down the pulp and obtain a larger volume of juice 10. In general, he considered that ripe fruits contain hardly any toxins, unlike seeds 11.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat think of orange juice compared with soft drinks and bread?
Peat noted that orange juice has a lower glycemic index than white bread, and that it would be inconsistent to advise against the juice while recommending the bread 3. In addition, for him the juice supplies minerals such as potassium, which refined starches lack 2.
Why did Peat say fruit should be eaten with protein?
He held that protein alone stimulates insulin and can lower blood sugar, which triggers adrenaline and cortisol, and that cortisol destroys part of the protein by turning it into sugar and fat 6. With fruit, according to him, that stress is avoided and the protein is used better 1.
Why did Peat distrust apples and underripe fruit?
According to Peat, underripe fruit contains a lot of pectin and little sugar, and people cannot digest it but bacteria can, so to a large extent it feeds the intestinal flora 8. For him, gas is a sign that a food is not being digested well 4.
How much orange juice and milk did Peat suggest?
In one of his articles he noted that a diet with about two quarts of milk and one of orange juice a day supplies enough sugars to resist stress in general 7. He added that larger amounts of juice or honey could protect against greater stress 7.
