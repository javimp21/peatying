Ray Peat on cholesterol

According to Peat, cholesterol is not a harmful substance but one of the body's most important defensive molecules, and conventional medicine is wrong to treat it as an enemy 12. For him, cholesterol is the raw material of the protective steroid hormones, and high levels usually indicate that the body is defending itself from a problem, above all a low thyroid metabolism 32.

Cholesterol as a protective molecule

Peat held that cholesterol is, together with glucose, probably the most versatile protective molecule we have, and that it rises during stress because it is the basis of the anti-stress hormones 2. In his opinion, it has an anti-inflammatory effect in itself and protects against radiation and various toxins 1.

He also counted it among the structural antioxidants, together with vitamin E, progesterone and vitamin D, which according to him prevent oxidation partly by stabilising molecular structures. One of its basic functions would be to stabilise the mitochondria against stress 1.

From cholesterol to progesterone and other steroids

For Peat, cholesterol is the precursor of all the body's steroids, including pregnenolone, progesterone and DHEA 14. He described an experiment in which, when the cholesterol in the blood reaching an ovary was increased, the progesterone produced increased in proportion 14.

According to him, that conversion needs enough thyroid and vitamin A 14. Progesterone and pregnenolone would have a general protective function: antioxidant, anticonvulsant, anticoagulant, anticancer and good for memory, among others 3. That is why he argued that any interference with cholesterol formation affects all those functions 3.

High cholesterol, thyroid and metabolism

Peat recalled that since the 1930s it had been known that a lack of thyroid function raises serum cholesterol, and that restoring thyroid hormone brought it back to normal 35. According to him, metabolism and cholesterol move as mirror images: when the metabolic rate falls, cholesterol rises 52.

He interpreted this to mean that thyroid doesn't curb cholesterol synthesis but favours its use to form hormones and bile salts 3. With a slow metabolism, the body would raise cholesterol to keep up progesterone production 2. He told of two people with very high cholesterol who, taking T3 in physiological doses, brought it down to about 200 mg/dl in a week 5. For him, high cholesterol was also a reliable diagnostic sign of hypothyroidism 61.

Atherosclerosis: inflammation and thyroid, not cholesterol

Peat stated that the thyroid's protection against atherosclerosis isn't due to lowering cholesterol but to other mechanisms 1. He recalled that rabbits fed cholesterol developed atherosclerosis, but that they were an exception and that not even they developed it if they were given thyroid 1. He added that when rabbits had their thyroid removed, they suffered atherosclerosis 7.

For him, the problem is inflammatory in nature. He mentioned intestinal endotoxin, which a healthy liver normally holds in check, and HDL lipoproteins, which protect by binding to those endotoxins 1. He also criticised medicine for focusing on the fats in atheromatous plaque and not on the whole process, which includes clotting, vascular spasms, heart rhythm, blood viscosity and deposits of calcium and iron 1.

Cholesterol-lowering drugs

According to Peat, artificially lowering cholesterol proportionally reduces the ability to make progesterone, and this would explain the adverse effects and the increase in overall mortality in people who use these drugs 24. He cited a large Hungarian study from the mid-1980s in which lowering cholesterol with a drug was associated with more deaths from almost all causes, such as cancer or suicide 14.

On statins, he pointed out that they sometimes cause muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis), which he attributed not only to interference with coenzyme Q10 but simply to the lack of cholesterol, and which according to him could also affect the heart 82. He also believed that polyunsaturated oils lower cholesterol somewhat through a toxic effect, by making the liver retain it, and that when drugs or these oils were used to lower it, mortality rose 3.

Brain, mood and ageing

Peat linked the rise in accidents and suicides with low cholesterol to a possible deficiency of neurosteroids, which according to him depresses mood and impairs learning 1. He cited the Framingham study, in which people with cholesterol below 200 mg/dl had worse cognitive results 1, and stated that people over 50 with 200 or more had a lower risk of Alzheimer's 8.

Regarding ageing, he held that in young people a high metabolism quickly converts cholesterol into steroids and keeps it low in the blood, while with age the rise would probably be a spontaneous regulatory process that supports the synthesis of protective steroids, especially in the brain and retina 1. That is why he considered higher cholesterol acceptable in older people, because their tissues need more care 4.

Frequently asked questions

Is cholesterol bad according to Ray Peat?
No. For Peat cholesterol is a protective, cell-stabilising molecule, as well as the precursor of progesterone, pregnenolone and DHEA 12. He held that what doctors think they know about it is wrong 1.
Why does cholesterol rise according to Peat?
According to him, it rises as a defence against stress or when the thyroid is working poorly and metabolism is low, to keep up progesterone production 2. He also thought the rise with age is a spontaneous regulatory process 14.
What did Peat think of statins?
He held that lowering cholesterol with drugs reduces the ability to produce protective steroids and is associated with more mortality 21. He also mentioned muscle breakdown as a recognised side effect, which according to him can affect the heart 8.
What link did Peat see between cholesterol and the thyroid?
For him, thyroid hormone speeds up the use of cholesterol to produce hormones and bile salts, so cholesterol and the metabolic rate are mirror images 35. That is why high cholesterol seemed to him a sign of hypothyroidism 67.
