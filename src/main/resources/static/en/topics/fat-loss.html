Ray Peat on losing fat

For Ray Peat, losing fat was not about eating as little as possible, but about keeping the metabolic rate high. He held that fasting and very restrictive diets drive it down and make you lose mostly muscle 12. He also argued that counting calories ignores how the composition and timing of food change metabolism 3.

Why drastic diets slow the metabolism

According to Peat, the stress of a strict diet pushes the body to draw on its stored fats. Released as free fatty acids, those fats slow the metabolism and displace the use of glucose as a fuel 4. For him, this is a useful short-term adaptation, because little glucose is stored and otherwise the body's proteins would be consumed right away 4.

He described how, two or three days into a diet, the metabolic rate falls so much that by 10 or 14 days very little weight has been lost 2. He attributed part of that slowdown to stored polyunsaturated fat. When mobilised, it would block thyroid function in proportion to how unsaturated it is 2.

You lose muscle, not fat

Peat cited a study of a two-week fast in which about 95% of the weight lost came from protein-rich tissues, such as muscles and glands, and not from fat 1. With a low-calorie but moderate diet, by contrast, the loss is slower and consists mostly of fat 12. So he considered fasting probably unjustified as a way to lose weight 1.

His reasoning was that fat has a very slow metabolism. Someone who loses muscle has less active tissue to burn it and finds it harder and harder to lose weight 1. For him, building muscle and lymphatic tissue makes it easier to lose weight later, even if it means going up a little on the scale at first 1.

Chronic dieting and the adapting body

Peat described a study in which women who said they gained weight on 1,000 calories a day were admitted and what they actually ate was counted. Some kept their weight on 700 calories, especially those who had dieted many times 5. He explained this by the adaptations to a chronic, poorly balanced diet: the thyroid slows down and the muscles waste away from stress 5.

He compared stress metabolism with diabetic metabolism, in which fat is burned instead of sugar 5. If enough sugar or foods convertible into sugar are eaten, the body does not need to break down its own tissues to feed the brain and red blood cells 5. He added that resting muscles need hardly any glucose 5.

More than calories: sugar, PUFA and metabolism

Peat rejected the idea that a calorie is a calorie. He held that, in many people, 100 calories of sugar are not equivalent to 100 of protein, because of the specific dynamic action of protein and because of insulin secretion 3. For him, avoiding stimulating the pancreatic beta cells tends to leave energy available for normal functions instead of storing it as fat 3.

He also argued that the ability to oxidise sugar affects weight. According to him, people with obesity, like older people, oxidise sugar less well 4. He described an experiment with a diet of sugar and milk in which the subject's metabolic rate rose from below normal and his weight fell from 152 to 138 pounds 4. According to Peat, polyunsaturated fats lower the metabolism and especially hinder the use of sugars 4.

Calorie restriction and polyunsaturated fats

Peat pointed out that calorie restriction extends the life of many species, but that in animals it usually preserves the high metabolic rate of youth 6. He added that, in one study, cutting only polyunsaturated fats while keeping calories the same gave the same protective effects as a very low intake 7. According to him, when eating very little those fats are burned for energy and are not left free to cause damage 7.

From this he concluded that people with a high metabolism suffer less from polyunsaturated fats, because they burn them 7. He also mentioned a 1987 finding according to which removing toxic heavy metals from the diet had the same effect as calorie restriction 8. As an example of the limits of eating little, he cited Roy Walford, who limited his diet to about 1,600 calories for 30 years and developed ALS 6.

The thyroid, hormones and obesity

For Peat, low thyroid is central. He held that it can raise estrogen and cortisol, which favour fat formation, and that fat cells themselves can become a chronic source of estrogen 9. He described obesity as a mix of Cushing-type symptoms and disturbed stress hormones, because without thyroid energy the physiology does not balance 10.

He said that before 1950 obesity was among the reasons to suspect hypothyroidism 11. He criticised the fact that doctors were later taught to blame it on laziness and gluttony 1210. In his opinion, carefully adjusting thyroid, progesterone, DHEA and pregnenolone makes it possible to keep a high metabolism, with a higher body temperature and more calories burned 13. He also pointed out that eating carbohydrate stimulates the conversion of T4 into T3, so many extra calories of carbohydrate can be eaten without gaining weight 14.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Ray Peat say fasting doesn't work for weight loss?
According to Peat, in a two-week fast about 95% of the weight lost comes from muscles and glands, not fat 1. In addition, the metabolic rate falls within a few days because of the effect of free fatty acids on the thyroid 2. With less active tissue, losing weight becomes harder and harder 1.
What did Ray Peat think of counting calories?
He rejected the idea that a calorie is a calorie. He held that the form and timing of food affect insulin, appetite and metabolism, and that 100 calories of sugar can act very differently from 100 of protein 3. Even so, he considered it important not to eat excess calories while keeping body temperature high 13.
What link did Ray Peat see between the thyroid and obesity?
For him, a low thyroid can increase estrogen and cortisol, which favour fat formation 9. He also criticised blaming obesity on gluttony rather than on a hormonal problem 1012. He considered that good thyroid function helps keep the metabolism high 13.
What role do polyunsaturated fats play in losing fat, according to Peat?
He held that, when released from body stores, polyunsaturated fats interfere with thyroid hormone and slow the metabolism in proportion to how unsaturated they are 2. He also stated that reducing them, while keeping calories the same, produced protective effects comparable to those of a very low-calorie diet 7.
