Ray Peat on estrogen

For Ray Peat, estrogen is not the female hormone of youth but a stress hormone produced by damaged, ageing or energy-starved tissues 12. He held that with age the problem is usually not a lack of estrogen but an excess of it, without the balance of progesterone 13. That is why he criticised the idea that there is an estrogen deficiency that needs correcting 4.

A stress hormone, not only a female one

According to Peat, any tissue under stress starts producing estrogen, and he considered this a very basic reaction to strain 2. That is why he described it as a stress hormone made by any tissue in that situation, and he pointed out that older men produce amounts comparable to those of women their age 1.

He also criticised the way estrogen is defined by its effects on the uterus and the breast. For him, calling it the female hormone drew attention away from its many functions in men and created confusion about its anti-fertility effects and other toxicities 5.

Tissues as estrogen factories

Peat explained that the enzyme aromatase, which makes estrogen, exists throughout the body: in fat, skin, smooth muscle, breast, uterus, liver, brain and bone, among others 3. According to him, its activity increases with age and with cortisol, prolactin and prostaglandins, and it is curbed by progesterone, thyroid, aspirin and altitude 3.

From this he concluded that neither menopause nor removal of the ovaries protects against estrogen, because any irritated or low-energy area becomes an estrogen factory, in men and women alike 6. He cited experiments with monkeys in which the arms produced at least as much estrogen as the ovaries 6, and he held that removing the gonads in animals increases tissue aromatases 3.

Estrogen held in the tissues, and blood tests

For Peat, a blood test showing little estrogen doesn't prove a deficiency. When progesterone is sufficient, he held, it destroys the proteins that bind estrogen inside cells and releases it into the blood, where it can be controlled and eliminated 4. According to him, low blood estrogen can mean there is an excess inside the cells 4.

Drawing on measurements by other researchers, he described how the difference in concentration between tissue and plasma is smaller in the luteal phase, when progesterone is high, and how without progesterone tissues can contain 20 to 30 times more estrogen than plasma 3. In ageing, the fall in progesterone creates a situation similar to the follicular phase, which lets tissues concentrate estrogen even if the serum level is low 3.

Progesterone as a counterweight, and menopause

Peat saw progesterone as the natural complement to estrogen 7. He held that the failure of progesterone is what triggers menopause, and that when its monthly peak stops, what remains is an estrogen peak that is no longer balanced 1. He added that menopause itself results from prolonged exposure to estrogen since puberty, and that unopposed action after menopause must be even more harmful 3.

According to him, the estrogen/progesterone ratio is highest around ovulation and in the premenstrual week, and its disturbances coincide with the ages when thyroid disorders are most common 8. He also stated that stress can block the synthesis of progesterone and raise estrogen, and that a lack of protein in the diet makes it harder for the liver to eliminate estrogen 8.

The harm he attributed to excess estrogen

Peat recalled that endometrial and breast cancer were the first two cancers associated with uninterrupted exposure to estrogen 9. According to him, women with cycles without ovulation or with little progesterone developed overgrowth of the endometrium and were the most prone to that cancer 9. He added that excess estrogen makes it impossible for the uterus to sustain a pregnancy 9.

He also held that estrogen activates phospholipase A2 and so amplifies the toxicity of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA), an effect that grows with age and with less thyroid and progesterone 10. In his opinion, estrogen also raises cortisol and free fatty acids, and without the block provided by progesterone, stress syndromes appear, including abdominal fat 11. He acknowledged, however, that with everything in balance it increases fat on the thighs and hips and is essential for breast growth 11.

The estrogen industry, according to Peat

Peat stated that from the 1930s the pharmaceutical industry promoted estrogen as the female hormone because estrogenic substances were plentiful and cheap, while progesterone was expensive and had no synthetic equivalents 9. According to him, it was already known then that it could cause miscarriage, inflammation and cancer, and some of the best-known researchers warned about its dangers 9.

He told how at that time it had been seen that soot is estrogenic and that whatever produces the estrogenic effect also produces inflammation and cancer, through a cellular excitation that blocks oxidative energy production 12. He claimed that this was covered up when the industry convinced doctors that estrogen would prevent infertility and ageing 12. He also considered a famous experiment by Jensen, which held that estrogens are not altered metabolically, to be false, and said it served to sideline those who studied their complex action 13.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat think of estrogen?
He considered it a stress hormone produced by damaged, ageing or low-energy tissues, not the hormone of youth 12. He held that its excess, unopposed by progesterone, is associated with inflammation, cancer and ageing 96.
Does estrogen fall with age according to Ray Peat?
According to Peat, not necessarily: he cited studies in which serum estrogen levels rise in women from adolescence until past forty, and stressed that they also rise in older men 14. He also stated that even if the blood level was low, tissues could retain a lot of estrogen when progesterone is lacking 3.
Why did Peat say there is no such thing as estrogen deficiency?
Because, for him, any injured or ageing tissue becomes a source of estrogen through aromatase 43. He also held that the absence of estrogen in the blood can indicate an excess inside the cells 4.
What role did Peat give progesterone against estrogen?
He saw it as the natural complement to estrogen 7. According to him, it curbs aromatase, stops tissues from concentrating estrogen and curbs the rise in cortisol that estrogen causes 311.
