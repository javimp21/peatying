Ray Peat on salt

According to Peat, salt isn't the enemy it is thought to be: he held that restricting it is usually more harmful than eating it, because sodium helps maintain blood volume and curb stress hormones such as aldosterone 12. For him, together with protein, progesterone and thyroid, salt is one of the keys to good brain function 3.

How the fear of salt took hold

Peat explained that the idea of limiting salt gained strength around 1950, when diuretics came onto the market. Since these drugs removed sodium along with water, it was concluded that simply reducing dietary sodium would achieve a similar effect 41.

According to him, the practice was applied even to pregnant women, and some doctors and most vets already saw that it was very harmful. He held that, despite this, it became the norm for years and harmed millions of babies, and that salt restriction ended up as a cultural cliché 1.

Salt, aldosterone and thyroid

Peat stated that if sodium is restricted, the body produces more aldosterone, an adrenal hormone that retains sodium and makes you lose potassium and magnesium 523. For him, eating enough sodium was the simplest way to curb it, more so than an inhibitor drug 2. He added that by avoiding aldosterone, the kidneys conserve more magnesium, so eating a bit more salt would amount to taking more magnesium 3.

According to Peat, when the thyroid is underactive, through ageing or stress, the body easily loses sodium in the urine. That raises aldosterone, which over time causes higher blood pressure and fibrosis and degeneration of the blood vessels and heart 6. He also said that the carbon dioxide produced thanks to the thyroid helps retain sodium 3.

Sodium, albumin and oedema

Peat explained that sodium ions associate with the albumin in the blood, and that combination draws a lot of water into the vessels. This maintains blood volume and prevents excess fluid from staying in the tissues 5. With little sodium, he held, albumin leaks out of the vessels together with the water 5.

So, for him, sometimes eating enough salt is all it takes for albumin to hold water in the blood, for example in someone with swollen feet or with a puffy face and eyelids on waking. He said that people on a low-salt diet are very prone to water leaving the vessels 7.

Pregnancy and preeclampsia, according to Peat

Peat considered preeclampsia a state of generalised inflammation. He drew on the evidence of Tom Brewer, Douglas Shanklin and Jay Hodin, according to which the cause was malnutrition and it was corrected with enough protein, salt and calcium 5. For him, restricting salt in pregnancy was an important factor in causing it 5.

He explained that with little sodium, blood volume falls and the kidneys receive less blood flow, which activates renin and pushes the blood to circulate at higher pressure 15. He mentioned studies in which adding salt lowered women's blood pressure and prevented complications 1. He also recalled an experiment in which a little over 20 grams of extra salt a day were given and the symptoms of toxaemia disappeared almost immediately 7.

Premenstrual water retention and sleep

After reading Brewer, Peat saw similarities between premenstrual syndrome and preeclampsia, such as oedema, insomnia, cramps, high blood pressure and salt cravings. That is why he suggested to women with premenstrual water retention that they salt their food to taste and follow the craving instead of avoiding it 54. According to his account, the first woman who tried it was rid of the problem in the very first month, and several years of similar results made him think it worked 45.

Peat linked both situations to high estrogen, which according to him causes sodium loss and leaky capillaries 8. He also told how, eating all the salt they wanted, people of any age with sleep problems slept better. He cited studies in which salt restriction increased adrenaline, and one in which elderly people on a low-sodium diet suffered insomnia and returned to normal when they ate salt normally 5.

Blood pressure: what Peat disputed

Peat pointed out that, according to a review of about a hundred studies, restricting salt lowers blood pressure by a few points, but this doesn't usually translate into better health. In a study of 3000 people over four years there was more mortality among those who ate less salt 1. He also cited another of more than 11,000 people over 22 years that showed an inverse relationship between salt intake and mortality 1.

For him, the cause of high blood pressure had more to do with calcium. He told how David McCarron observed, using official figures, that people who ate less salt had higher blood pressure, and concluded that calcium deficiency was the main factor. According to Peat, that deficiency raises parathyroid hormone, which in turn raises aldosterone 9. He added that a low-salt diet increases free fatty acids and contributes to insulin resistance and atherosclerosis 5.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ray Peat think of salt?
Peat advocated salting food to taste and considered restricting it harmful. He held that sodium helps maintain blood volume and avoid oedema, and that a lack of it raises stress hormones such as aldosterone 152.
Why did Peat say a lack of salt raises aldosterone?
According to him, when the body loses sodium, the adrenals produce aldosterone to retain it. He held that if you eat enough sodium, aldosterone falls, and that keeping it high over time damages the vessels and the heart 56.
What link did Peat see between salt and fluid retention?
For Peat, water retention isn't caused by eating too much salt, but by the fact that with little sodium albumin can't hold water in the vessels and it leaks into the tissues. That is why he proposed salt for premenstrual or pregnancy oedema 754.
What caused high blood pressure according to Peat?
Peat held that it wasn't excess sodium. He pointed to a lack of calcium, which raises parathyroid hormone and aldosterone 9. He also spoke of low blood volume, which forces the blood to circulate at higher pressure 51.
