Ray Peat on insulin and diabetes

For Ray Peat, diabetes is not caused by sugar but by free fatty acids, especially polyunsaturated ones (PUFA), which keep cells from using glucose 12. He held that these fatty acids damage energy production in the mitochondria as well as the pancreatic cells that make insulin 34. So he considered the dietary obsession with sugar a dangerous detour in understanding these diseases 3.

The central idea: fats, not sugar

According to Peat, decades ago some physiologists in Israel understood that what causes diabetes is free fatty acids and not sugar. These fatty acids are produced when the body is under stress 1. He added that an Argentine researcher, in studies done almost in passing, had seen that sugar protected against diabetes 2.

For him, Bernardo Houssay's experiments in the 1940s, in which sugar and coconut oil protected against diabetes, and Randle's later demonstration that fats and glucose compete with each other, pointed in that direction 3. To this he added the growing recognition that PUFA cause insulin resistance and damage the pancreas 3.

How fats block the use of glucose

Peat explained that, according to Randle, the higher free fatty acids rise, the less capacity there is to use glucose. As an example he cited hospital patients given an intravenous soybean oil emulsion: within about 15 minutes, their ability to metabolise glucose practically disappeared 2. He considered this the instant diabetogenic effect of fatty acids 2.

According to him, free fatty acids slow mitochondrial respiration, so the cell relies more on glycolysis and produces lactic acid 4. In diabetics, the frequently high concentration of lactic acid would indicate that glucose does enter the cell but cannot be used oxidatively 5. The lactate travels to the liver, which turns it into more glucose 5. Moreover, one of the points where fatty acids block the use of glucose is its conversion into fructose within glycolysis 6.

Damage to the pancreas and blood vessels

Peat held that even brief exposure to PUFA can damage the insulin-secreting cells of the pancreas and the mitochondria, and that prolonged exposure causes progressive damage 3. If it persists, not only is glucose use in the tissues blocked, but the pancreatic beta cells are overloaded and poisoned 2. So the same toxic effects would explain the insulin resistance of type 2 diabetes and, by damaging or killing beta cells, an insulin deficiency similar to type 1 4.

He also pointed out that free PUFA increase capillary permeability, something detectable at the onset of insulin resistance. With chronic exposure the leakage grows, albumin appears in the urine, and the retina, brain and other organs are damaged 3. He recalled, too, that the first contraceptive pills produced signs of diabetes, and that in 1966 it was proposed that the cause might be a rise in free fatty acids 7.

Sugar and fructose in diabetes

Peat recalled that in the 19th century some doctors treated diabetes by adding sucrose to the diet, and that in 1874 it was published that diabetics assimilate fructose better than glucose. After the discovery of insulin in 1922, that fructose therapy was practically forgotten 68.

According to his explanation, fructose can bypass the block that fatty acids impose on glucose and keep feeding oxidative metabolism 6. He cited a study according to which diabetics have less fructose than normal in their serum, and concluded it was reasonable to supply it in the diet 6. He also said that in some studies sugar stimulated the regeneration of pancreatic beta cells 2. Even so, he admitted that anyone who eats more than they need gains weight 1.

Fats he considered protective

For Peat, PUFA are toxic to the cells' energy-producing system, and diabetes would be just one of the terminal diseases that polyunsaturated vegetable oils can cause. In his opinion, coconut oil is very protective against it 7.

He explained that, if there is no excess of PUFA, the body can desaturate some of the saturated fats it makes from carbohydrate or gets from foods such as chocolate or beef. In this way it produces the omega-9 series, which he considered anti-inflammatory and protective against plant-derived omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids 9. Oleic acid, the main fat in avocado and olive oil, belongs to that series 9.

His criticism of the mainstream medical view

Peat stated that believing that eating sugar causes diabetes is a pre-scientific idea that probably most doctors still hold. He recalled that Claude Bernard discovered in 1848 that the body makes sugar from tissue proteins 6. He also explained that the name diabetes was extended to obese people with high glucose once measuring blood sugar became widespread 6.

He pointed out that, despite insulin, other drugs and campaigns to change diet, mortality from diabetes has increased over the last century, and type 1 is growing even among children 4. According to him, a culture was built around diabetes and sugar that benefited various industries, including the insulin industry, and ended up presenting seed oils, fish oils and other products as healthy 3.

Frequently asked questions

Does sugar cause diabetes according to Ray Peat?
No. Peat held that what causes diabetes is free fatty acids, particularly polyunsaturated ones, and not sugar 12. For him, believing otherwise is a pre-scientific idea still widespread among doctors 6.
What is insulin resistance for Peat?
According to Peat, it results from free fatty acids, especially PUFA, slowing mitochondrial respiration and glucose oxidation. So glucose cannot be used even though it enters the cell 54. He added that the toxic effects of PUFA explain the insulin resistance of type 2 diabetes 4.
Why did Peat defend fructose and sucrose in diabetes?
He held that fructose can bypass the block fatty acids place on glucose and still be oxidised 6. He also recalled that since 1874 it had been known that diabetics assimilate it better than glucose, and that diabetes was once treated with sucrose 68.
What did Peat say about coconut oil and PUFA in diabetes?
For him, PUFA are toxic to cellular energy production and can contribute to causing diabetes, while coconut oil is very protective 7. He also cited Houssay's experiments, in which coconut oil and sugar protected against diabetes 3.
