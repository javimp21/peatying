Ray Peat on testosterone and DHEA

For Ray Peat, testosterone and DHEA belong to a group of protective steroids that help the body resist stress, together with pregnenolone and progesterone 1. He held that when those steroids are scarce the body tends to make more estrogen and cortisol, and that this is why the system should not be thrown off balance with excessive amounts of androgens 21.

Why testosterone falls with age

According to Peat, older men have low testosterone because their stressed tissues quickly turn it into estrogen, a process called aromatization 3. He pointed out that estrogen rises in men with age, just as it rises with stress, illness, malnutrition and hypothyroidism 4.

He added that estrogen is produced in fat, which tends to increase with the years, just when thyroid hormone and progesterone are lacking 4. At 50, he said, many men show an excess of prolactin and estrogen and a deficit of thyroid and testosterone 4. He also stated that men produce estrogen mostly under stress, for example in starvation, alcoholism or liver damage 5.

Testosterone and the prostate

Peat criticized the idea that estrogen opposes testosterone and that testosterone causes the prostate to grow and turn cancerous. According to him, everything in that sentence is wrong, even though each part of the belief appears in the medical literature 4. He also noted that prostate growth in old age is very different in nature from that of youth 4.

He cited experiments with slices of human prostate in which insulin and probably prolactin stimulated cell division, while testosterone did not 4. In his judgment, there were signs that supplemental androgens might even control prostate cancer, and that antagonists of prolactin and estrogen would be useful in hormonal therapy 4.

A group of protective steroids

Peat held that the first barrier against damage is the set formed by pregnenolone, progesterone, DHEA and testosterone, whose protective effects largely overlap 1. With plenty of these hormones, he said, the body withstands efforts that would be dangerous for a frail person. When they are lacking, anything becomes stressful and the body turns to emergency cortisol production 1.

In his opinion, testosterone works together with progesterone and pregnenolone so that there is less need to make stress hormones such as cortisol 6. He also described testosterone as a hormone with chemical and functional properties of both estrogen and progesterone, which would give the male short-term stability but less capacity for long-term adaptation 5.

Where each hormone comes from

For Peat, pregnenolone is the precursor of progesterone, DHEA and the rest of the steroid hormones 78. He said that progesterone cannot be turned into testosterone or estrogen, because it follows the path toward glucocorticoids such as cortisol, while the precursor of testosterone and estrogen is DHEA 9.

He also explained that with age the renewal of the adrenal glands slows down and the zone that produces androgens and progesterone breaks down, so that the proportion of cells producing aldosterone and cortisol increases 10. He added that the active thyroid hormone (T3) falls with the years, which reduces the production of pregnenolone and progesterone. According to him, DHEA can rise under stress as a result of the lack of thyroid, progesterone and pregnenolone 4.

What DHEA does and the risk of excess

Peat gave DHEA a role similar to that of thyroid hormone: it would stimulate cells to absorb and burn sugar, increase heat production and help prevent obesity 8. He said that young people produce about 12 to 15 milligrams a day and that this amount falls by about 2 milligrams per decade after the age of 30 8.

He insisted on not overdoing it. He told of some older men who took DHEA and saw their estrogen rise to the level of a young woman in mid-cycle, with an enlarged liver 2. According to him, excess DHEA can be converted into estrogen or testosterone, especially when progesterone, pregnenolone or thyroid are deficient, and those large amounts would disturb the thymus and the liver 8. He applied the same logic to androgens in general: a young man produces about 12 milligrams of DHEA and 4 or 5 of testosterone a day, and going beyond those amounts raises estrogen and backfires 2.

Why he preferred pregnenolone

Peat recounted that he stopped recommending DHEA because people felt so good that they took more and more, until one person ended up with an enlarged liver and the estrogen level of a teenage girl 11. He went on to recommend pregnenolone to almost everyone, which in his judgment is the safest option 211.

He relied on experiments with rats, in which an enormous dose of pregnenolone did not alter the hormones of healthy animals but lowered the stress hormones in stressed animals 11. He himself took about 3,000 or 4,000 milligrams a day for a year and said he noticed a great deal of resistance to stress, with no side effects 211. He also argued that normalizing the stress hormones with pregnenolone usually corrects thyroid function, probably because stress suppresses it 7.

Frequently asked questions

Why does testosterone fall with age, according to Ray Peat?
According to Peat, older men have little testosterone because stressed tissues rapidly convert it into estrogen 3. He also noted that estrogen is produced in fat, which tends to increase with age when thyroid and progesterone are lacking 4.
Can the body turn progesterone into testosterone?
Peat believed it cannot. According to him, progesterone is a precursor of the glucocorticoids, such as cortisol, but it does not follow the path toward testosterone or estrogen, whose precursor is DHEA 9.
What did Peat think of taking DHEA?
He saw DHEA as a youth-associated hormone that supports the use of glucose and the production of heat 8. However, he stopped recommending it because, as he told it, some people took more and more and ended up with very high estrogen and an enlarged liver, and he preferred pregnenolone 11.
Does testosterone cause prostate cancer, according to Peat?
Peat rejected that belief. He cited experiments in which testosterone did not stimulate the division of human prostate cells, while insulin and probably prolactin did 4. For him, prostate growth in old age is very different from that of youth 4.
