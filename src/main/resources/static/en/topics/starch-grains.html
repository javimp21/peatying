Ray Peat on starch and grains

For Ray Peat, cooked starch is digested and absorbed almost as fast as pure glucose, which is why it triggers a strong surge of insulin 1. He held that this response favours fat formation, hormonal stress and appetite 2. So, whenever better options such as fruit were available, he preferred not to base the diet on grains 2.

A starch absorbed almost like glucose

Peat described a classic laboratory experiment: a rat was given ten grams of cornstarch paste and, ten minutes later, none was left in its digestive tract. For him this showed how quickly starch is digested and absorbed 12. He added that on glycemic index tables starch sits very high, at a level similar to pure glucose 1.

He criticised David Jenkins, who in 1981 measured the rise in blood glucose over the first two hours. According to Peat, that measure ignores the physiological processes that determine how much glucose is in the blood at any moment 2. Even so, he believed that work showed starch to be more glycemic than sucrose 2.

Insulin, hormonal stress and appetite

Peat thought the harm of starch depends less on sustained high blood sugar than on the stress reaction that follows a strong insulin stimulus. When insulin lowers blood sugar, hormones such as cortisol are activated, and they in turn promote the breakdown of the body's own proteins 2. According to him, if there is surplus glucose that cannot be oxidised right away, insulin turns it into fat 34.

He also held that starch, by raising insulin and then lowering blood sugar, stimulates appetite and makes people eat more, so the effect on fat is multiplied 2. For him, sucrose works differently: only half of it is glucose and fructose slightly inhibits insulin, so it supplies energy more gradually 32. He added that if starch is eaten with polyunsaturated fats (PUFA), which hinder its oxidation, even more fat is produced 4.

Fruit versus bread and pasta

Peat stated that eating complex carbohydrates instead of sugars was a reasonable way to promote obesity 2. He pointed out that eating bread and pasta is associated with diabetes, while eating fruit is inversely associated with it 2. For him, the differences between fruit and grains are not just about their effect on blood sugar 2.

He qualified this, however: there is nothing wrong with a high-carbohydrate diet, and a high-starch diet need not be incompatible with good health. What he advocated was using better foods when they are available 2. He also said that some potatoes and some white rice have a glycemic index almost double that of white sugar 5.

Undigested starch, bacteria and fibre

According to Peat, starch that is not digested reaches the bowel and feeds bacteria. He cited studies with semi-digestible so-called prebiotic starches in which the animals became anxious and aggressive. He added that some products of this bacterial action can greatly increase endotoxin and inflame the intestine, the liver and, over time, the whole body 1.

He also explained that the phosphate in seeds and grains stimulates the bacterial breakdown of fibres, while calcium slows it down. So, for him, a diet rich in calcium and low in phosphate, together with saturated fats, suppresses the toxic effects of starches 6. In one of his books he added that starch can stimulate allergic responses, block small vessels and accelerate ageing, according to the work of G. Volkheimer and others 7.

Grain protein and grain toxins

For Peat, the protein value of grain is negligible, because seeds store their protein in a form that is very rich in nitrogen but poor in essential amino acids 8. He held that seeds contain defensive toxins and need special preparation to reduce them 8. By contrast, according to him, fruits, roots and tubers provide many nutrients with few toxic antimetabolic substances 8.

He also compared protein quality on Norman Curie's scale: rice at around 6% of the 100% of egg yolk, and potato slightly above the yolk 9. He considered potato protein some fifteen or twenty times better than that of rice or beans 9.

Soaking, fermenting and nixtamalising

Peat explained that leaving dough moist for 8 to 12 hours activates enzymes, like those of sprouting. Phytic acid breaks down into inositol and phosphate, and gluten is partly broken down 10. According to him, fermented bread comes closer to a sprout, made mostly of protein and water with some sugar, than to the grain with its starch and high phosphate 6.

He criticised the hurried baker for depriving the population of much of the grain's protein and making a somewhat toxic product. He added that iodate and bromate, which inhibit the thyroid, are added to most bakery bread 11. The exception he saw was corn treated with alkali, as in tortillas, which he considered relatively harmless and more digestible. He recalled that pellagra was associated with ordinary corn, not with the traditional preparations 7.

His criticism of dietitians' advice

Peat considered that the advice to eat many daily servings of bread, cereals and other starches never had a scientific justification. He attributed it to a widespread dietary culture in the United States 2. He suggested that the dietitians' association's links with breakfast cereal companies might influence those opinions 2.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Ray Peat say starch is more fattening than sugar?
According to Peat, starch turns into glucose very quickly and strongly stimulates insulin, which promotes fat synthesis when there is surplus glucose 34. Sucrose is only 50% glucose and its fructose slightly inhibits insulin, so for him it tends to store less fat 32. He added that starch increases appetite and makes people eat more 2.
What did Peat think of the glycemic index?
He acknowledged that starch has a very high glycemic index, similar to glucose 14. But he criticised Jenkins's method, which only measures blood glucose for two hours and ignores the physiological processes involved 2. For him, the differences between fruit and grains are not explained by blood sugar alone 2.
What did Peat think of fermented or sourdough bread?
According to him, soaking and fermenting activate enzymes that break down phytic acid and part of the gluten 10. The more the grain is digested, the more it resembles a sprout, with more protein and less indigestible starch 6. He criticised hurriedly made bread, which in his view loses much of the grain's protein 11.
Was there any grain Peat considered relatively harmless?
Yes: corn processed traditionally with alkali, as in tortillas, which for him is more digestible and less linked to pellagra than ordinary corn 7. He also said a high-starch diet is not necessarily incompatible with good health, although he preferred other foods when possible 2.
