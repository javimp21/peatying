Ray Peat's raw carrot salad

For Ray Peat, a salad of grated raw carrot was an everyday food that cleaned the bowel, reduced the absorption of bacterial endotoxin and kept the estrogen excreted in bile from being reabsorbed 12. He held that, because of these effects, it could resemble an antibiotic treatment without its side effects 1.

The recipe according to Peat

The basic version he described has grated carrot, vinegar, coconut oil and salt; garlic and olive oil were optional 3. Elsewhere he explained that olive or coconut oil, with a dash of vinegar without additives, improves the flavour, and that it could be varied with lime, cheese or meat 14.

He recommended grating the carrot lengthwise, because the length of the fibre matters, and not putting it in a blender, so that much of the fibre stayed intact 4. Even so, he found raw, moderately grated carrot pleasant enough to eat every day 2.

A natural antiseptic for the bowel

According to Peat, carrots grow underground and have to resist moulds and bacteria, so they contain harmless germicidal substances that act as a disinfectant in the bowel 25. As evidence, he pointed out that an old carrot takes weeks to go mouldy and hardly ever rots 6.

He held that, since it is not digested, the carrot carries the oil and vinegar along the bowel, which for him are fungicidal and bactericidal, so the whole acts as a broad-spectrum antiseptic 5. The fibre would work as a slow-release system that also binds toxins and stimulates the bowel 3.

Endotoxin and estrogen reabsorption

Peat explained that the liver inactivates estrogen and that part of it goes into the bile. If fibre is lacking, that estrogen can be reabsorbed and return to the liver, and with an excess of estrogen the liver slows down and loses its capacity to detoxify it 2. For him, fibre binds the bile and its estrogen content and prevents its reabsorption 2.

In another text he qualified that the antiestrogenic effect of fibre seems to come mainly from speeding up bowel transit 7. He also recounted that several women with premenstrual symptoms whose serum estrogen was measured before and after the carrot diet saw it fall within a few days, while their symptoms improved 7.

Bowel transit and constipation

For Peat, the bulk of the carrot stimulates the bowel and shortens transit time if one is eaten every day. He cited a survey according to which the average American took about three days and the average African less than one 2. He added that a good metabolism and thyroid function favour fast peristalsis, while a sluggish thyroid slows the elimination of estrogen 8.

In another text he stated that a carrot a day, as a snack or grated, can prevent almost all constipation. He added that its fibre holds on to fat molecules and makes weight loss easier, and that its small amount of sugar is released slowly 9.

Symptoms and hormones he linked to the carrot

Peat stated that many people saw premenstrual syndrome, headaches or allergies disappear with daily use of raw carrot 1. He himself said that eating a raw carrot a day had prevented his migraines 7. For premenstrual syndrome he made it his first suggestion, along with avoiding thyroid suppression 3.

He also thought it could help normalise progesterone, by protecting against intestinal absorption of endotoxin and reducing estrogen reabsorption. According to him, the hormonal effects of antibiotics (more progesterone, less cortisol and estrogen) could be achieved with this salad in most cases 10. He did warn, however, that an excess of carotene can inhibit progesterone synthesis 1011.

Why not just any salad

Peat acknowledged that any fibre has some antiestrogenic effect, but considered raw carrot a very safe option 2. He admitted that carrots have their own toxins, although, raw and moderately grated, very little of them is absorbed 8. As alternatives he mentioned well-cooked mushrooms or bamboo shoots and well-cooked oat bran 8.

By contrast, he advised against raw leaves such as lettuce. He recounted that, when he put it in a sealed bag at about 90 degrees Fahrenheit to imitate the bowel, it rotted in two or three days with heavy bacterial growth, and that since we have no enzymes to break down cellulose something similar would happen when eating it 2.

Frequently asked questions

How did Ray Peat make the carrot salad?
According to Peat, the raw carrot is grated, preferably lengthwise and without blending it, and dressed with a little vinegar without additives, coconut or olive oil, and salt 43. Garlic was optional, and to vary the flavour he allowed lime, cheese or meat 13.
Why did Peat recommend raw carrot for the bowel?
He held that carrots contain antiseptic substances they use to defend themselves against moulds and bacteria in the soil, and that these disinfect the bowel 25. In addition, its indigestible fibre carries oil and vinegar through the bowel and stimulates transit 25.
What link did Peat see between carrots and estrogen?
For him, carrot fibre binds the estrogen in bile and keeps it from being reabsorbed, so the liver is not overloaded 2. He recounted that in women with premenstrual symptoms serum estrogen fell within a few days on the carrot diet 7.
Did Peat think a lettuce salad would do instead of carrot?
No. According to Peat, raw leaves such as lettuce rot with heavy bacterial growth under conditions similar to those of the bowel, and humans cannot digest cellulose 2. So he advised sticking to raw carrot to eliminate endotoxins 2.
